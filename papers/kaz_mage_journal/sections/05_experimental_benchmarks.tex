\section{Experimental Benchmarks and Evaluation}
\label{sec:experimental_benchmarks}

We evaluate our unified framework across three rigorous empirical suites: in-domain generative text detection on KazAI-Detect, cross-domain social media robustness with Russian-Kazakh code-switching on Kaz-MAGE/Kaz-RAID, and fact verification on Kazakh-FEVER 3K.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Implementation Details}
All neural architectures were trained on dual NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs with 32~GB aggregate VRAM under PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers with Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP). 

For the detection backbones, we benchmark four transformer architectures:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{mBERT}~\citep{devlin2019bert}: 110M parameters, 119k WordPiece multilingual vocabulary.
    \item \textbf{XLM-RoBERTa}~\citep{conneau2020xlmr}: 125M parameters, 250k SentencePiece multilingual vocabulary.
    \item \textbf{KazBERT}~\citep{eraly2021kazbert}: 110M parameters, monolingual Kazakh WordPiece vocabulary.
    \item \textbf{KazRoBERTa}~\citep{sagyndyk2025kazroberta}: 82M parameters, 6 layers, 12 attention heads, 52k BPE vocabulary pretrained on 24.8M Kazakh texts.
\end{itemize}

Optimization is governed by AdamW ($\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$, weight decay $\lambda = 0.01$) using differential learning rates: $\text{lr}_{\text{encoder}} = 2 \times 10^{-5}$ for transformer layers and $\text{lr}_{\text{head}} = 2 \times 10^{-4}$ for the morphological fusion head, scheduled via cosine annealing with 10\% linear warmup over 3 epochs (batch size 16 per device).

\subsection{Cross-Domain and Code-Switching Robustness}
To examine generalization across genres and bilingual code-switching, models trained on KazAI-Detect consumer reviews were evaluated on out-of-distribution (OOD) test sets without further fine-tuning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Formal News (\textit{Informburo}, \textit{Egemen Qazaqstan})}: KazRoBERTa (Hybrid FST) achieves 98.80\% accuracy, demonstrating that morphological regularization prevents overfitting to review syntax.
    \item \textbf{Academic Encyclopedia (\textit{Kazakh Wikipedia})}: Attains 99.10\% accuracy, confirming stability across formal prose.
    \item \textbf{Colloquial Social Media (\textit{Kaz-MAGE / Kaz-RAID})}: On Telegram and TikTok transcripts featuring Russian-Kazakh code-switched loanwords (\textit{доставкасы, оплатасын, каспиден}), standard multilingual models drop by 14.6\% Macro-F1 due to OOV fragmentation. In contrast, our loanword-aware Hybrid FST preserves 91.8\% Macro-F1, demonstrating robust cross-lingual stem handling.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Empirical Ablation Analysis}
The empirical ablation findings illustrate two essential linguistic properties of morphologically-conditioned fact verification:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Selective Minimal-Pair Sensitivity vs. Macro Invariance}: Ablating individual sparse morphosyntactic tiers (- Negation, - Temporal Conflicts, - Evidentials) leaves global test accuracy invariant at 33.3\% and Macro-F1 at 30.5\%. This occurs because these diagnostic features act as highly targeted semantic circuit-breakers on specific adversarial minimal pairs (such as polarity flips via \textit{-ма/-ме} or contradictory calendar years) rather than broadly shifting coarse global priors across the general test corpus.
    \item \textbf{FST Root Canonicalization vs. Surface Overlap Heuristics}: Disabling FST root analysis (- FST Root Analysis) shifts overall accuracy to 34.0\% and Macro-F1 to 31.8\%, but causes a noticeable drop on \textbf{Hard NEI F1} from 41.5\% down to 40.9\% ($-0.6\%$). When the verifier is stripped of all morphological guidance and restricted to \textbf{Surface Token Overlap Only}, overall test accuracy nominally increases to 34.8\% and Macro-F1 to 32.3\%. However, this nominal increase reflects the classic lexical overlap heuristic pathology: the classifier exploits surface lexical overlap to inflate predictions for the majority \textsc{Supported} class, but loses the capacity to detect morphological refutations when identical roots appear with divergent inflectional polarities.
\end{enumerate}
