\subsection{Error Analysis across Kazakh Affixation Chains}
We examine linguistic failure modes across three canonical categories of Kazakh morphological constructions:

\noindent\textbf{1.~Negation Suffix Interference:} In Kazakh, verbal negation is mediated by unstressed allomorphs \textit{-ба/-бе/-па/-пе/-ма/-ме} directly adjacent to the verbal root (\textit{барған} [went] vs.\ \textit{бармаған} [did not go]). Subword tokenizers partition polymorphemic verbs into fragments that obscure this boundary, leading pretrained encoders to predict \textsc{Supported} when surrounding nominal arguments match evidence. Baseline XLM-RoBERTa exhibited a 38.3\% error rate across 120 negated test instances, whereas our morphology-aware verifier reduced errors to 12.5\%.

\noindent\textbf{2.~Indirect Evidentiality and Epistemic Modality:} Kazakh grammar marks direct witnessed past events (\textit{-ды/-ді}) distinct from indirect reportative or inferential events (\textit{-ыпты/-іпті}, particle \textit{екен}). Claims asserting direct certainty when evidence expresses speculative modality should be classified as \textsc{Not Enough Info}. Standard models treat evidential markers as stylistic variants rather than truth-conditional operators, whereas our model conditions on explicit evidential features ($m_5, m_6$).

\noindent\textbf{3.~Case-Agreement and Syntactic Role Inversion:} As an SOV language, Kazakh marks grammatical relations via case suffixes: accusative (\textit{-ны/-ні}), dative (\textit{-ға/-ге}), locative (\textit{-да/-де}), and ablative (\textit{-дан/-ден}). When claims invert agent-patient roles while retaining identical stems, bag-of-words and naive dense encoders fail to detect contradiction, whereas our model preserves case alignments through explicit feature encoding~($m_{13}$).

\subsection{Qualitative Case Studies}
Table~\ref{tab:case_studies} illustrates representative examples from Kazakh-FEVER 3K. In Case 2, the claim inserts negative suffix \textit{-ба-} and emphatic adverb \textit{мүлде}, inverting polarity while maintaining 88\% lexical stem overlap. XLM-RoBERTa predicts \textsc{Supported} due to entity overlap; our system detects the negation affix and correctly predicts \textsc{Refuted}. In Case 3 (Hard NEI), the claim introduces ungrounded epistemic speculation (\textit{болуы мүмкін}) and universal quantification (\textit{барлық ... өзі ғана}). Our verifier recognizes that the modal qualification lacks evidential warrant, correctly predicting \textsc{Not Enough Info}.

\subsection{Interactive Explainability and Deployment}
\label{sec:explainability}
To facilitate transparent auditing and human-in-the-loop verification, an interactive web demonstration and code repository are available for peer review,\footnote{Online demo and anonymized repository: \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/kazakh-ai-text-detector}} and the complete interactive environment and evaluation scripts are included in the supplementary materials archive. Developed with Gradio, the interface provides sub-second verification with bilingual localization in Kazakh (\textit{қазақ тілі}) and English.

The interface maps submitted documents into the continuous 2D trust space $\mathcal{T} = [-1, 1] \times [0, 1]$ via a client-side procedural SVG Cartesian plane (Fig.~\ref{fig:trust_matrix}). The horizontal axis measures factual veracity $T_{\text{fact}} \in [-1, 1]$ (Equation~\ref{eq:trust_fact}) and the vertical axis measures generative authenticity $T_{\text{gen}} \in [0, 1]$ (Equation~\ref{eq:trust_gen}), partitioning predictions into four operational quadrants with real-time HUD telemetry for composite trust $\mathcal{T}(x)$ (Equation~\ref{eq:trust_score}). A runtime model toggle between \textit{Ours (Hybrid + Morpho)} and \textit{Baseline (Surface Only)} demonstrates how suffix manipulations confound models lacking morphological features.

Underneath the executive trust card, each atomic claim is displayed in a structured table with expandable diagnostic drawers exposing the full 16-dimensional vector $\mathbf{m}_{\text{affix}} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ (directional negation mismatch $\Delta_{\text{neg}}$, calendar/numerical conflicts $\Delta_{\text{year}}, \Delta_{\text{num}}$, evidentials, FST root Jaccard $J_{\text{root}}$ vs.\ surface overlap $J_{\text{surf}}$, and case alignment). Crucially, the top evidence passage incorporates real-time matched stem highlighting: all lexical roots shared with the claim (identified by our 83-rule FST analyzer) are visually rendered with distinctive highlighting, providing interpretable evidential grounding during high-stakes auditing.
