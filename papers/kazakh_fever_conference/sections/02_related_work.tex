\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Fact Verification in Low-Resource Languages}
Fact verification benchmarks have expanded substantially since the foundational FEVER challenge~\citep{thorne2018fever}, which formalized verification as evidence retrieval over Wikipedia followed by natural language inference (NLI) classification into \textsc{Supported}, \textsc{Refuted}, or \textsc{Not Enough Info} (NEI). Subsequent benchmarks incorporated multi-hop tabular reasoning~\citep{aly2021feverous} and real-world claim styles~\citep{schuster2019fever}. While cross-lingual question answering and verification benchmarks such as TyDi QA~\citep{clark2020tydi} and multilingual NLI corpora have broadened language coverage, Central Asian languages remain critically underserved. Prior fact-checking corpora in non-Indo-European languages have almost universally overlooked agglutinative morphology, yielding datasets where lexical overlap heuristics dominate baseline model performance. Kazakh-FEVER 3K directly addresses this void by introducing gold evidence alignments grounded in Kazakh encyclopedic and journalistic texts.

\subsection{Morphologically-Aware Turkic NLP}
The Turkic language family, comprising over 35 distinct languages, is characterized by extensive agglutinative affixation and strict vowel harmony. Early computational work on Kazakh centered on rule-based finite-state transducers (FSTs) and morphological analyzers~\citep{makhambetov2013kazakh,bekmanova2021kazakh}. With the ascent of large-scale pretrained transformers, multilingual models such as mBERT~\citep{devlin2019bert} and XLM-RoBERTa~\citep{conneau2020xlmr} have achieved strong empirical results across many languages. However, these models employ generic subword tokenizers that frequently segment Kazakh inflectional chains into phonologically and semantically arbitrary fragments. Recent work highlights that incorporating explicit morphological segmentation and morpheme boundary inductive biases is essential to mitigate data sparsity and stem distortion in agglutinative NLP~\citep{guo2026kazakh}. Our architecture builds upon these insights by unifying morpheme-aware sparse retrieval with dense representations.

\subsection{AI-Generated Text Detection and Trust Modeling}
The release of high-capacity generative language models~\citep{touvron2023llama} has lowered the barrier to generating plausible yet factually incorrect assertions. Existing synthetic text detection frameworks primarily evaluate statistical perplexity curvature, such as DetectGPT~\citep{mitchell2023detectgpt}, or train supervised classifiers on neural representations. Nevertheless, prior work predominantly treats synthetic text detection as an isolated binary classification task. In real-world media environments, human-written texts can be factually deceitful, while machine-generated texts may synthesize verified facts accurately. Treating veracity and generative origin as independent dimensions is therefore critical for holistic trust calibration. Our 2D Trust Matrix resolves this dichotomy by projecting veracity and authenticity predictions into a unified epistemic coordinate system.
