\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we introduced \textbf{Kazakh-FEVER 3K}, the first human-validated, morphologically-grounded fact verification benchmark for the Kazakh language. Kazakh-FEVER 3K comprises 3,000 rigorously annotated claim-evidence pairs spanning canonical encyclopedic assertions and emerging journalistic claims. To inoculate models against superficial lexical heuristics, we formulated the Hard NEI protocol, systematically demanding fine-grained morphological reasoning over negation, evidentiality, and modality markers.

To address the challenges of agglutinative vocabulary expansion, we developed a hybrid evidence retrieval pipeline combining morpheme-stemmed BM25 with dense multilingual representations via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, achieving an 84.6\% Recall@5 over 250,000 Wikipedia passage chunks. Furthermore, we designed a morphological NLI verifier augmented with explicit affix feature embeddings, paired with a 2D Trust Matrix that unifies factual veracity with synthetic text origin detection. Our experimental results demonstrate that our architecture outperforms state-of-the-art multilingual baselines, achieving an overall Macro-F1 of 82.1\% and an 11.5\% absolute improvement on the Hard NEI split.

In future work, we plan to extend this framework along two key frontiers: first, scaling cross-lingual evidence retrieval across closely related Turkic languages (such as Kyrgyz, Uzbek, and Uyghur) through shared morphological representations; second, broadening the verification pipeline to handle multi-modal evidence streams, including manipulated social media imagery and synthesized audio broadcasts.
