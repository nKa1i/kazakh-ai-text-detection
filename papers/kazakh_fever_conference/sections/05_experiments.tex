\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate our proposed pipeline on the full Kazakh-FEVER 3K benchmark using the curated Kazakh Wikipedia passage repository containing 250,000 indexed chunks.

\paragraph{Baselines}
We compare against standard multilingual and generative baselines across both retrieval and verification stages:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Retrieval}: Standard Lucene BM25~\citep{robertson2009bm25}, BM25 with rule-based morphological stemming, multilingual Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR)~\citep{karpukhin2020dpr}, and unsupervised multilingual Contriever (\textsc{mContriever})~\citep{izacard2021contriever}.
    \item \textbf{Verification}: Pretrained multilingual encoders fine-tuned on our training split, including mBERT-base~\citep{devlin2019bert}, XLM-RoBERTa-base, and XLM-RoBERTa-large~\citep{conneau2020xlmr}. In addition, we evaluate LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct~\citep{touvron2023llama} under a zero-shot instruction prompt formulated in Kazakh.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation Details}
All transformer verifiers are trained for 5 epochs using AdamW with an initial learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-5}$, linear warmup over the first 10\% of steps, batch size of 16, and weight decay of 0.01 on NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Early stopping is triggered based on development set Macro-F1. For evaluation, we compute standard 3-class Accuracy, Macro-averaged F1, the official FEVER Score (which requires correct label classification conditioned on retrieving at least one gold evidence sentence), and dedicated F1 on the Hard NEI evaluation subset.

\subsection{Main Results}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the main verification results. Our morphologically-grounded pipeline achieves 82.6\% accuracy and 82.1\% Macro-F1, outperforming XLM-RoBERTa-large by +5.2\% absolute Macro-F1 and +6.6\% FEVER score. Crucially, on the Hard NEI split, baseline models suffer substantial degradation: mBERT-base drops to 48.2\% F1 and XLM-RoBERTa-large achieves only 61.3\% F1. In contrast, our model maintains 72.8\% F1 on Hard NEI, indicating that explicit morphological conditioning prevents the model from defaulting to naive lexical overlap heuristics.

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_results} demonstrates the clear superiority of hybrid retrieval over standalone sparse or dense models. Standard BM25 attains a Recall@5 of 70.4\% due to vocabulary mismatch across Kazakh affixations. Adding morphological stemming increases Recall@5 to 76.8\% (+6.4\%), while our hybrid combination with \textsc{mContriever} via RRF reaches 84.6\% Recall@5 and 0.718 MRR, establishing a robust evidence foundation for downstream verification.

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}
To quantify the individual impact of each architectural component, we conduct extensive ablation experiments:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Impact of Morphological Stemming in Retrieval}: Replacing morpheme-stemmed BM25 with raw surface BM25 in the hybrid retriever decreases Recall@5 from 84.6\% to 78.2\% (a 6.4\% absolute drop), demonstrating that agglutinative variations must be resolved before lexical frequency scoring.
    \item \textbf{Impact of Dense Semantic Indexing}: Removing the dense \textsc{mContriever} channel degrades Recall@5 by 7.8\% (to 76.8\%), showing that semantic paraphrase understanding is essential when claims diverge syntactically from evidence.
    \item \textbf{Impact of Affix Feature Embeddings in Verifier}: Removing the morphological feature vector $\mathbf{m}_{\text{affix}}$ from the NLI verifier yields a 9.1\% reduction in Hard NEI F1 (falling from 72.8\% to 63.7\%). This confirms that explicitly representing negation and modal affixes is vital for detecting subtle fact alterations that deceive standard attention pooling.
\end{enumerate}
