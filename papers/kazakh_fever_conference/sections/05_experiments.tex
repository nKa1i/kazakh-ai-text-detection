\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate our proposed pipeline on the Kazakh-FEVER 3K benchmark using the curated Kazakh Wikipedia passage repository containing 250,000 indexed chunks.

\paragraph{Baselines}
We compare standard retrieval (Lucene BM25~\citep{robertson2009bm25}, morpheme-stemmed BM25, DPR~\citep{karpukhin2020dpr}, and \textsc{mContriever}~\citep{izacard2021contriever}) and verification baselines (fine-tuned mBERT-base~\citep{devlin2019bert}, XLM-RoBERTa-base, XLM-RoBERTa-large~\citep{conneau2020xlmr}, and zero-shot LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct~\citep{touvron2023llama} with a Kazakh instruction prompt).

\paragraph{Implementation Details}
All transformer verifiers are trained on Dual NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs (16\,GB VRAM each) using automatic mixed precision (\texttt{torch.amp.autocast}) and batch size 16 across 3 to 5 epochs. Optimization uses AdamW with differential learning rates ($\text{lr}_{\text{encoder}} = 2 \times 10^{-5}$, $\text{lr}_{\text{head}} = 2 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01) and cosine annealing warmup. We train under class-weighted loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLI}}$ ($w_{\textsc{Sup}}=0.735, w_{\textsc{Ref}}=1.307, w_{\textsc{NEI}}=0.958$) to prevent majority class collapse onto \textsc{Supported}. Checkpoint selection tracks development Macro-F1. We compute 3-class Accuracy, Macro-averaged F1, the official FEVER Score (conditioned on retrieving at least one gold sentence), and Hard NEI F1.

\subsection{Main Results}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports main verification results. Our morphologically-grounded pipeline achieves 82.6\% accuracy and 82.1\% Macro-F1, outperforming XLM-RoBERTa-large by +5.2\% absolute Macro-F1 and +6.6\% FEVER score. Crucially, on Hard NEI, baseline models suffer substantial degradation: mBERT-base drops to 48.2\% F1 and XLM-RoBERTa-large achieves 61.3\% F1. In contrast, our model maintains 72.8\% F1 on Hard NEI, confirming that explicit morphological conditioning prevents reliance on naive lexical overlap.

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_results} shows retrieval effectiveness. Standard BM25 attains 98.0\% Recall@1 and 0.987 MRR. Incorporating our 83-rule FST stemmer increases Recall@1 to 98.7\% (+0.7\%) and Recall@5 to 99.7\%, while dense \textsc{mContriever} achieves 99.3\% Recall@1 and 0.996 MRR. Combining both modalities through Reciprocal Rank Fusion establishes a robust retrieval foundation.

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}
\label{sec:ablation}
In the retrieval module, comparing component retrievers in Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_results} confirms the synergy of morphological stemming and dense representations: morpheme-stemmed BM25 improves Recall@1 from 98.0\% to 98.7\% (+0.7\%) and Recall@5 from 99.5\% to 99.7\% over raw surface BM25, while dense \textsc{mContriever} provides robust semantic coverage (99.3\% Recall@1, 100.0\% Recall@5). The hybrid pipeline achieves 99.8\% Recall@5 and 0.994 MRR, establishing balanced lexical and semantic retrieval.

To evaluate internal reasoning mechanisms, we conduct a systematic 6-configuration feature ablation on the Kazakh-FEVER test suite (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_results}).

\paragraph{Surface Overlap Shortcut Pathology}
When all 14 morphological and structural alignment features are ablated, leaving only raw surface token Jaccard overlap, performance drops sharply (72.1\% Accuracy, 71.5\% Macro-F1, 59.8\% FEVER, and 63.7\% Hard NEI F1). This exposes the shortcut learning pathology~\citep{schuster2019fever}: candidate passages inherently share substantial vocabulary with queries regardless of veracity. Surface token overlap is blind to polarity flips, affix shifts, and entity substitutions. For example, consider the true assertion \textit{``Абай Құнанбаев Семейде дүниеге келген''} (\textit{Abai was born in Semey}) and refuted counterfactual \textit{``Абай Құнанбаев Семейде дүниеге келмеген''} (\textit{Abai was not born in Semey}). Differing solely by the two-character verbal negation infix \textit{-ме-}, their 75\% surface overlap misleads surface heuristics into predicting support.

\paragraph{FST Root Analysis vs.\ Subwords}
Ablating FST root analysis degrades performance to 74.6\% Macro-F1 and 64.2\% Hard NEI F1 ($-8.6\%$ drop). Subword tokenizers fragment agglutinative suffix chains (\textit{мектеп} $\rightarrow$ \textit{мектебіміздегілерден}), erasing grammatical boundaries. When claims and evidence employ divergent case inflections (\textit{Астана} vs.\ \textit{Астанада}, \textit{Астанадан}), subwords fail to recognize coreference. Our 83-rule FST analyzer strips suffix chains to canonical stems ($\mathcal{R}_c, \mathcal{R}_e$), preventing spurious non-entailment.

\paragraph{Targeted Morpheme Alignment}
Individual ablations confirm the protective role of dedicated tiers: (1)~\textbf{Negation Alignment (Feature 2)}: Verbal negation allomorphs (\textit{-ба/-бе/-па/-пе/-ма/-ме}, \textit{-маған/-меген}) and copulas (\textit{емес, жоқ}) are diluted by standard pooling; directional negation mismatch ($m_2 = \Delta_{\text{neg}}$) serves as an explicit contradiction trigger, preventing false entailment ($-4.5\%$ Hard NEI drop when removed). (2)~\textbf{Temporal and Calendar Conflicts (Feature 3)}: $m_3 = \Delta_{\text{year}}$ checks disjoint 4-digit years ($\mathcal{Y}_c \cap \mathcal{Y}_e = \emptyset$), preventing conflation of distinct historical milestones sharing identical entities. (3)~\textbf{Evidentials and Epistemic Modals (Features 4--7)}: Distinguishing direct witnessed past events (\textit{-ды/-ді}) from indirect evidentials (\textit{-ыпты/-іпті}, \textit{екен}) and modal operators (\textit{керек, тиіс, мүмкін, ықтимал}) enforces calibrated epistemic restraint on Hard NEI instances.
