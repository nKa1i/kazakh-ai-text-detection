\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:literature_review}

The intersection of artificial intelligence text detection, factual verification, and computational morphology represents an active research frontier in modern natural language processing. This section surveys international and domestic research across these foundational domains, examining architectural paradigms, linguistic challenges, benchmark case studies, and core research directions.

\subsection{Design and Architecture of AI Text Detection Systems}
\label{sec:design_architecture}

Automated machine-generated text detection frameworks have evolved through multi-tiered architectures comprising four primary functional stages: ingestion, representation modeling, classification and aggregation, and verification interface integration.

Early algorithmic designs evaluated intrinsic token probability distributions, $n$-gram perplexity curvatures, rank-order statistics, and token burstiness under language model priors \cite{wang2024raid}. These detectors leverage the observation that autoregressive decoding strategies (top-$p$ nucleus sampling, top-$k$ truncation) disproportionately select high-probability tokens, yielding uniformly flat perplexity trajectories compared to the bursty, variable distribution of human prose.

With pretrained transformer encoders, supervised neural classifiers became the dominant paradigm \cite{vaswani2017attention, sagyndyk2025kazroberta}. Supervised detectors frame detection as binary sequence classification, employing cross-entropy or contrastive objectives to learn discriminative stylistic representations from labeled corpora. However, static supervised classifiers suffer from distribution shift: models trained on specific generators collapse when evaluated on unseen instruction-tuned foundation models \cite{wang2024raid, khurana2023contrastive}.

To alleviate reliance on static training distributions, recent research explores zero-shot, perturbation-based detection. Mitchell et al.\ \cite{mitchell2023detectgpt} introduced \textsc{DetectGPT}, positing that machine-generated passages occupy negative-curvature local optima within a model's log-probability landscape. By measuring directional log-likelihood divergence under T5 mask perturbations, DetectGPT enables zero-shot detection. Hans et al.\ \cite{hans2024binoculars} developed \textsc{Binoculars}, formulating detection as the cross-perplexity ratio between two foundation models, eliminating perturbation latency. Concurrently, Bao et al.\ \cite{bao2024fastdetectgpt} proposed \textsc{Fast-DetectGPT}, using analytical approximations of Hessian curvature to accelerate inference by three orders of magnitude. Furthermore, Mudunuri et al.\ \cite{mudunuri-etal-2026-bounded} demonstrated that bounded memory combined with evidence highlighting substantially elevates user trust during interactive verification.

\subsection{Overview of AI and Morphological Detection Approaches}
\label{sec:overview_ai_morpho}

In typologically complex agglutinative languages, standard neural modeling encounters severe linguistic mismatches. Computational modeling of agglutinative morphology has historically progressed along two separate traditions: formal symbolic rule engines and statistical subword tokenizers.

In the symbolic paradigm, Finite-State Transducers (FSTs) based on Koskenniemi's two-level morphology provide bidirectional mappings between surface wordforms and underlying grammatical representations \cite{tyers2012free, khoshhal2020kazakh}. The Apertium platform established open-source morphological analyzers for Kazakh, utilizing continuation lexicons to model vowel harmony and consonant assimilation \cite{tyers2012free, khoshhal2020kazakh}. While symbolic FSTs achieve near-perfect precision on grammatical derivations, their integration within deep learning has been hindered by non-differentiable execution boundaries and out-of-vocabulary brittleness.

In the statistical paradigm, unsupervised subword algorithms (BPE, SentencePiece) dominate language modeling. However, empirical studies expose severe shortcomings of subword tokenization in agglutinative contexts. Khurana et al.\ \cite{khurana2024subwords} demonstrated that subword tokenizers frequently partition grammatical affixes across arbitrary character boundaries, completely obscuring syntactic dependencies, case relations, and negation markers. This fragmentation degrades representation learning and limits transformer forensic sensitivity.

Concurrently, the broader NLP ecosystem for Kazakh has established vital foundational resources. Togmanov et al.\ \cite{togmanov-etal-2025-kazmmlu} introduced \textsc{KazMMLU}, evaluating language models across 56 subjects and highlighting performance gaps between English and regional languages. Laiyk et al.\ \cite{laiyk-etal-2025-instruction} demonstrated the efficacy of instruction tuning on curated public government and cultural corpora in Kazakh. In specialized representations, Yeshpanov and Varol \cite{yeshpanov2024kazsandra} constructed \textsc{KazSAnDRA} for fine-grained sentiment analysis, Sagyndyk et al.\ \cite{sagyndyk2025kazroberta} introduced \textsc{Kaz-RoBERTa} for contextual language understanding, and Tukenov \cite{tukenov2026sozkz} released \textsc{SozKZ}, training efficient small language models natively from scratch. Despite these advances, existing resources focus on general pretraining or sentiment classification, leaving hybrid neural-symbolic architectures for forensic text verification an urgent research necessity.

\subsection{Case Studies}
\label{sec:case_studies}

To ground our investigation in empirical benchmarking traditions, this section reviews two pivotal case studies that illustrate contemporary evaluation paradigms and diagnostic pathologies.

\subsubsection{Case 1: Cross-Generator AI Text Detection}
\label{sec:case1_cross_generator}

A fundamental challenge in forensic text analysis is whether a detector can identify text synthesized by foundation models absent from the training set. This phenomenon was systematically investigated by the \textsc{RAID} benchmark \cite{wang2024raid}. Evaluating detection across eleven LLMs (GPT-4, LLaMA-3, Mistral, Cohere), RAID demonstrated that supervised neural detectors overfit to idiosyncratic generator-specific stylistic artifacts, collapsing by 30 to 50 percentage points in AUROC under Leave-One-Generator-Out (LOGO) evaluation. These findings underscore the necessity of a 2$\times$2 evaluation matrix: (1) Domain Transfer: In-Domain (news) vs.\ Out-of-Domain (reviews); and (2) Generator Transfer: In-Generator vs.\ Out-of-Generator. However, existing benchmarks focus almost exclusively on English, providing zero diagnostic coverage for Kazakh.

\subsubsection{Case 2: Fact Verification Frameworks}
\label{sec:case2_fact_verification}

Automated fact verification gained widespread prominence with the Fact Extraction and VERification (FEVER) benchmark by Thorne et al.\ \cite{thorne2018fever}. The standard FEVER paradigm decomposes verification into three stages: (1) \textit{Document Retrieval} from an authoritative corpus via sparse (BM25 \cite{robertson2009bm25}) or dense retrievers (\textsc{M3-Embedding} \cite{chen-etal-2024-m3}); (2) \textit{Sentence Evidence Extraction}; and (3) \textit{Natural Language Inference (NLI)}, predicting: \textsc{Supported}, \textsc{Refuted}, or \textsc{Not Enough Info} (NEI) \cite{wang-etal-2024-factcheck-bench}.

However, verification models frequently succumb to \textit{shortcut learning} \cite{wang-etal-2024-factcheck-bench}. Because standard \textsc{NEI} instances pair claims with unrelated passages, verifiers exploit shallow lexical overlap heuristics. In agglutinative languages, altering a two-character negative suffix reverses truth value while retaining over 80\% surface token overlap. Without an adversarial ``Hard NEI'' protocol, standard models suffer catastrophic failure. Furthermore, no native fact verification benchmark exists for Kazakh, highlighting a critical research gap.

\subsection{Main Research Points}
\label{sec:main_research_points}

Building upon these literature insights, this dissertation formulates three interlocking research points that define our core methodological agenda (illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep]
    \item \textbf{Point 1: Resolving Subword Tokenization Fragmentation via Dual-Stream Gated Fusion.} Standard tokenizers arbitrarily partition Kazakh words, destroying morphological affixes. We develop a dual-stream architecture combining an 83-rule symbolic FST morphological parser with a pretrained Kaz-RoBERTa transformer encoder, fused via dynamic cross-attention gating to ensure morphologically grounded representation learning.
    
    \item \textbf{Point 2: Mitigating Cross-Generator Shift via Sentence-Preserving Chunk Pooling.} Existing detectors overfit to generator artifacts and fail under localized splicing. We introduce a sentence-preserving chunking mechanism with 10 abbreviation regex guards and dynamic top-$k$ extreme value pooling, ensuring length-agnostic forensic sensitivity and robust invariance across unseen LLM generators.
    
    \item \textbf{Point 3: Decoupling Generative Origin from Veracity via Kazakh-FEVER and a 2D Trust Matrix.} AI text detection and fact verification operate in disjoint silos. We establish the Kazakh-FEVER 3K benchmark with a dedicated Hard NEI protocol, and unify veracity and provenance onto a continuous 2D Cartesian Trust Matrix $(T_{\text{fact}}, T_{\text{gen}})$, providing human auditors with transparent trust calibration.
\end{itemize}
