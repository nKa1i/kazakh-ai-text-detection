\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:literature_review}

The intersection of artificial intelligence detection, factual verification, and computational morphology represents an active, rapidly evolving frontier in modern natural language processing. This section surveys current international and domestic research across these foundational domains, examining architectural paradigms, linguistic challenges, benchmark case studies, and core research directions.

\subsection{Design and Architecture of AI Text Detection Systems}
\label{sec:design_architecture}

Automated machine-generated text detection frameworks have evolved through multi-tiered system architectures comprising four primary functional stages: ingestion and preprocessing, representation modeling, classification and aggregation, and verification interface integration.

Early algorithmic designs operated predominantly on statistical metric pipelines, evaluating intrinsic token probability distributions, $n$-gram perplexity curvatures, rank-order statistics, and token burstiness under assumed language model priors \cite{solaiman2019release}. These metric-based detectors leverage the observation that autoregressive generation strategies (such as top-$p$ nucleus sampling and top-$k$ truncation) disproportionately select high-probability tokens from truncated distributions, yielding uniformly low perplexity trajectories compared to the bursty, variable distribution of human-authored prose.

With the advent of pretrained transformer encoders (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa, and XLM-RoBERTa), supervised neural classifiers emerged as the dominant paradigm \cite{devlin2019bert, conneau2020xlmr}. Supervised detectors frame detection as a binary sequence classification task, employing cross-entropy or contrastive objectives to learn discriminative stylistic representations directly from labeled human and machine corpora. However, static supervised classifiers suffer from severe distribution shift: classifiers trained on older generator architectures exhibit drastic performance drops when evaluated on unseen instruction-tuned foundation models \cite{wang2024raid, khurana2023contrastive}.

To alleviate reliance on static training distributions, recent research has explored zero-shot, perturbation-based detection paradigms. Mitchell et al.\ \cite{mitchell2023detectgpt} introduced \textsc{DetectGPT}, positing that machine-generated passages occupy negative-curvature local optima within a language model's log-probability landscape. By applying mask-filling perturbations via T5 and measuring directional log-likelihood divergence, DetectGPT enables zero-shot detection without fine-tuning. Hans et al.\ \cite{hans2024binoculars} developed \textsc{Binoculars}, formulating detection as the normalized ratio of cross-perplexity between two distinct pretrained foundation models, thereby removing the latency of stochastic perturbation sampling. Concurrently, Bao et al.\ \cite{bao2024fastdetectgpt} proposed \textsc{Fast-DetectGPT}, using analytical approximations of Hessian curvature to accelerate inference speeds by three orders of magnitude, and Yang et al.\ \cite{yang2023dnagpt} introduced \textsc{DNA-GPT} based on divergent $n$-gram analysis. Furthermore, weak supervision frameworks such as \textsc{Ghostbuster} \cite{verma2024ghostbuster} and adversarial learning frameworks such as \textsc{RADAR} \cite{hu2023radar} seek to enhance detector robustness against prompt perturbations.

At the interface and deployment tier, recent research demonstrates that automated black-box predictions are insufficient for high-stakes operational environments \cite{khashabi2021genie}. Real-world auditing requires bounded, verifiable evidence and interpretable attribution heatmaps; for example, Mudunuri et al.\ \cite{mudunuri-etal-2026-bounded} established that bounded memory combined with evidence highlighting substantially elevates user trust during interactive verification.

\subsection{Overview of AI and Morphological Detection Approaches}
\label{sec:overview_ai_morpho}

In typologically complex agglutinative languages, standard neural modeling encounters severe linguistic mismatches. Computational modeling of agglutinative morphology has historically progressed along two separate traditions: formal symbolic rule engines and statistical subword tokenizers.

In the symbolic paradigm, Finite-State Transducers (FSTs) based on Koskenniemi's two-level morphology framework provide exact, bidirectional mappings between surface wordforms and underlying grammatical representations \cite{tyers2012free, khoshhal2020kazakh}. The Apertium collaborative platform established open-source morphological analyzers for Kazakh, utilizing morphotactic continuation lexicons to model palatal and labial vowel harmony and consonant assimilation \cite{tyers2012free, makazhanov2015kazakh}. While symbolic FSTs achieve near-perfect precision on grammatical derivations, their application within modern deep learning has been severely hindered by non-differentiable execution boundaries and brittleness on out-of-vocabulary named entities.

In the statistical paradigm, unsupervised subword algorithms such as Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), WordPiece, and SentencePiece dominate contemporary language modeling. However, recent empirical investigations have exposed the severe shortcomings of subword tokenization in agglutinative contexts. Khurana et al.\ \cite{khurana2024subwords} demonstrated that subword tokenizers frequently partition grammatical affixes across arbitrary phoneme boundaries, completely obscuring syntactic dependencies, case relations, and negation markers. This fragmentation degrades representation learning and limits the forensic effectiveness of standard transformer backbones.

Concurrently, the broader NLP ecosystem for Kazakh and Central Asian languages has established vital foundational resources. The \textsc{Belebele} benchmark \cite{bandarkar-etal-2024-belebele} provided a parallel reading comprehension suite across 122 language variants including Kazakh. Togmanov et al.\ \cite{togmanov-etal-2025-kazmmlu} introduced \textsc{KazMMLU}, the first comprehensive evaluation of language models on Kazakh, Russian, and regional knowledge across 56 subjects, highlighting dramatic performance gaps between English and regional languages. Laiyk et al.\ \cite{laiyk-etal-2025-instruction} demonstrated the efficacy of instruction tuning on curated public government and cultural corpora in Kazakh. In specialized representations, Yeshpanov and Varol \cite{yeshpanov2024kazsandra} and Khairullina et al.\ \cite{khairullina2022kazsandra} constructed \textsc{KazSAnDRA} for fine-grained sentiment analysis, Sagyndyk et al.\ \cite{sagyndyk2025kazroberta} introduced \textsc{Kaz-RoBERTa} for contextual language understanding, and Tukenov \cite{tukenov2026sozkz} released \textsc{SozKZ}, training efficient small language models natively from scratch. 

Despite these advances, existing resources focus predominantly on general pretraining or sentiment classification. None systematically address the interaction between complex agglutinative suffix chains and multi-stage forensic text verification. Hybrid neural-symbolic architectures that directly inject formal morphological tag sequences into deep transformer representations remain an urgent, unexplored research necessity.

\subsection{Case Studies}
\label{sec:case_studies}

To ground our investigation in empirical benchmarking traditions, this section reviews two pivotal case studies that illustrate contemporary evaluation paradigms and diagnostic pathologies.

\subsubsection{Case 1: Cross-Generator AI Text Detection}
\label{sec:case1_cross_generator}

A fundamental challenge in forensic text analysis is whether a detector can identify machine-generated text synthesized by foundation models completely absent from the training set. This phenomenon was systematically investigated by the \textsc{RAID} benchmark \cite{wang2024raid} and machine-generated text (MAGE) evaluation frameworks.

The RAID benchmark evaluated detection performance across millions of generations spanning eleven contemporary LLMs, including GPT-4, LLaMA-3, Mistral, and Cohere architectures. Crucially, the study demonstrated that supervised neural detectors severely overfit to idiosyncratic generator-specific stylistic artifacts. Classifiers trained on a single generator family experienced performance collapses of 30 to 50 percentage points in AUROC when evaluated on out-of-distribution generators under a Leave-One-Generator-Out (LOGO) protocol. Furthermore, adversarial paraphrasing and domain shifting (e.g., from formal news articles to informal social media text) accelerated this degradation.

These findings underscore the necessity of evaluating detectors across a rigorous 2$\times$2 evaluation matrix spanning two orthogonal axes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Domain Transfer:} In-Domain (formal news) versus Out-of-Domain (consumer reviews).
    \item \textbf{Generator Transfer:} In-Generator (known training model) versus Out-of-Generator (unseen held-out model).
\end{itemize}
However, existing cross-generator benchmarks remain focused almost exclusively on English and high-resource languages, providing zero diagnostic coverage for morphologically complex agglutinative languages such as Kazakh.

\subsubsection{Case 2: Fact Verification Frameworks}
\label{sec:case2_fact_verification}

Automated fact verification gained widespread prominence with the release of the Fact Extraction and VERification (FEVER) benchmark by Thorne et al.\ \cite{thorne2018fever}. The standard FEVER paradigm decomposes verification into a three-stage sequential architecture: (1) \textit{Document Retrieval}, identifying relevant articles from an authoritative corpus via sparse (BM25 \cite{robertson2009bm25}) or dense retrievers (DPR \cite{karpukhin2020dpr}, Contriever \cite{izacard2022mcontriever}, M3-Embedding \cite{chen-etal-2024-m3}); (2) \textit{Sentence Evidence Extraction}, selecting the minimal evidentiary sentences sufficient to substantiate or refute the claim \cite{zheng-etal-2024-evidence}; and (3) \textit{Natural Language Inference (NLI)}, predicting a three-way veracity label: \textsc{Supported}, \textsc{Refuted}, or \textsc{Not Enough Info} (NEI).

Subsequent benchmarking expanded this framework to adversarial human attacks (FEVER 2.0 \cite{thorne2019fever2}), real-world multi-domain claims (MultiFC \cite{augenstein2019multifc}), scientific assertions (CLIMATE-FEVER \cite{diggelmann2020climatefever}), lightweight document-grounded verifiers (MiniCheck \cite{tang-etal-2024-minicheck}), and causal argument graphs (CheckWhy \cite{si-etal-2024-checkwhy}).

However, diagnostic studies have revealed that automatic fact verification models frequently succumb to \textit{shortcut learning} and spurious correlation exploitation \cite{wang-etal-2024-factcheck-bench, mitra-etal-2025-factlens}. In standard FEVER benchmarks, instances belonging to the \textsc{NEI} class are created by pairing claims with randomly sampled unrelated passages. Consequently, verifiers learn a shallow heuristic: low surface token overlap implies \textsc{NEI}, while high surface lexical overlap implies \textsc{Supported}. In agglutinative languages, this pathology is particularly dangerous: altering a two-character negative suffix reverses the factual truth of a claim while retaining over 80\% surface token overlap. Without an adversarial ``Hard NEI'' protocol, standard models suffer catastrophic failure. Furthermore, no dedicated native fact verification benchmark exists for Kazakh, highlighting a critical research gap.

\subsection{Main Research Points}
\label{sec:main_research_points}

Building upon the insights and limitations identified across the literature, this dissertation formulates three interlocking research points that define our core methodological agenda (illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Point 1: Resolving Subword Tokenization Fragmentation and Morphological Domain Collapse via Dual-Stream Gated Fusion.} Standard subword tokenizers arbitrarily partition agglutinative Kazakh words, destroying morphological affixes and causing severe performance collapse under cross-domain transfer. This research develops a dual-stream architecture combining an 83-rule symbolic FST morphological parser with a pretrained Kaz-RoBERTa transformer encoder, fused via dynamic cross-attention gating to ensure morphologically-grounded representation learning.
    
    \item \textbf{Point 2: Mitigating Cross-Generator Shift and Localized Document Tampering via Sentence-Preserving Chunk Pooling.} Existing detectors overfit to generator-specific surface regularities and fail when synthetic text is spliced into human-authored documents. This research introduces a sentence-preserving chunking mechanism coupled with top-$k$ extreme value pooling, ensuring length-agnostic forensic sensitivity and robust invariance across unseen foundation model generators.
    
    \item \textbf{Point 3: Decoupling Generative Origin from Factual Veracity via Kazakh-FEVER and a 2D Cartesian Trust Matrix.} AI text detection and factual verification have historically developed in disjoint silos, failing to differentiate between synthetic factual text, synthetic hallucinations, human facts, and human misinformation. This research establishes the Kazakh-FEVER 3K benchmark with a dedicated Hard NEI protocol, and unifies veracity and provenance onto a continuous 2D Cartesian Trust Matrix $(T_{\text{fact}}, T_{\text{gen}})$, providing human auditors with transparent, multidimensional trust calibration.
\end{itemize}
