\section{Research Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Relevance and Context}
\label{sec:relevance_and_context}

The rapid deployment of autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs)---including GPT-4, LLaMA-3, DeepSeek-R1, and multilingual foundation models such as Aya---has transformed automated reasoning and digital communication \cite{vaswani2017attention, achiam2023gpt4, dubey2024llama3, guo2025deepseekr1, ustun-etal-2024-aya}. While these models generate fluent, varied, and persuasive text across diverse domains, widespread access simultaneously escalates risks regarding information integrity, epistemic credibility, and cybersecurity \cite{achiam2023gpt4, dubey2024llama3}. Modern digital platforms confront synthetic artifacts ranging from automated disinformation and fraudulent consumer reviews to AI-assisted plagiarism and hallucinated pseudo-factual assertions delivered with unmerited confidence.

Consequently, establishing computational mechanisms for auditing digital text has become an essential requirement for trustworthy artificial intelligence. Reliable information verification demands two foundational capabilities: (1) \textit{AI-generated text detection}, which identifies generative origin by determining whether a candidate passage was composed by a human or synthesized by a neural generator; and (2) \textit{automated fact verification}, which evaluates whether factual assertions are substantiated by reputable reference evidence.

While text auditing has matured in high-resource, typologically analytic or mildly fusional languages like English and Chinese, existing methods encounter fundamental performance bottlenecks in low-resource, morphologically complex agglutinative languages \cite{khurana2024subwords, togmanov-etal-2025-kazmmlu}. Languages of the Turkic family---exemplified by Kazakh, spoken by over 15 million people across Central Asia---possess typological structures that directly challenge standard deep learning assumptions. In agglutinative morphology, lexical roots anchor sequential suffixation chains encoding syntactic case, tense, evidentiality, possessive relations, and negation within single polymorphemic words. A solitary verbal root produces hundreds of surface wordforms without introducing new headwords. The proliferation of multilingual models in regional communication ecosystems creates an urgent need for language-native auditing infrastructure.

\subsection{Challenges of Existing AI Detection Systems}
\label{sec:challenges_existing}

Despite substantial progress in machine-generated text detection, contemporary detection paradigms exhibit critical vulnerabilities when applied to Kazakh:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=(\arabic*), nosep]
    \item \textbf{Subword Tokenization Fragmentation:} Transformers rely on statistical subword tokenizers (BPE, WordPiece, SentencePiece) trained on high-resource multilingual corpora \cite{khurana2024subwords}. These algorithms optimize merges based on surface frequency rather than grammatical morphology. In agglutinative languages, standard tokenizers partition polymorphemic words across arbitrary character boundaries, fragmenting functional affixes into meaningless byte tokens. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:fst_fragmentation}, the Kazakh word \textit{мектебіміздегілерден} (``from those who are in our school'') contains a structured sequence of root, possessive, locative, adjectivizer, plural, and ablative morphemes. Standard tokenizers partition this word into disjoint subwords, obscuring both root lemma and grammatical markers, inflating sequence lengths, and attenuating gradient propagation.
    
    \item \textbf{Absence of Morphological Priors and Linguistic Grounding:} Conventional neural detectors treat text as unconstrained surface token sequences, lacking explicit inductive biases regarding root-affix compositionality, vowel harmony, and morphotactic suffix ordering \cite{khoshhal2020kazakh, tyers2012free}. Consequently, classifiers struggle to distinguish natural inflectional richness from synthetic generation artifacts, frequently misclassifying formal human prose as machine-generated text.
    
    \item \textbf{Severe Cross-Generator and Out-of-Domain Degradation:} Zero-shot probability curvature methods (DetectGPT \cite{mitchell2023detectgpt}, Binoculars \cite{hans2024binoculars}, Fast-DetectGPT \cite{bao2024fastdetectgpt}) rely on surrogate models to evaluate local log-likelihood curvatures. In low-resource agglutinative languages, surrogate models possess undertrained parametric vocabularies, causing unstable log-probability surfaces. Supervised classifiers overfit to generator-specific surface regularities \cite{wang2024raid, khurana2023contrastive}, experiencing collapses exceeding 30 percentage points in AUROC on unseen LLMs or out-of-domain distributions (e.g., news versus reviews).
    
    \item \textbf{Inability to Handle Localized Synthetic Tampering:} Conventional detectors use uniform truncation (first 512 tokens) or naive average pooling, assuming documents are entirely human-written or entirely synthetic. In real-world dissemination, deceptive texts often splice isolated synthetic sentences into authentic human articles. Global pooling dilutes localized forensic signals, leaving architectures incapable of detecting hybrid manipulations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Potential of Morphologically-Grounded AI and Verification}
\label{sec:potential_morpho_verification}

To overcome these architectural and typological limitations, this research proposes a morphologically-grounded computational framework unifying symbolic morphological modeling, deep transformer representations, and multi-stage factual verification.

First, integrating formal computational morphology via an 83-rule Finite-State Transducer (FST) provides bidirectional decomposition of agglutinative words into canonical roots and inflectional tags \cite{khoshhal2020kazakh, tyers2012free}. Unlike statistical subword tokenizers, the 83-rule FST explicitly models Kazakh vowel harmony, consonant assimilation, and morphotactic rules. Coupling this symbolic analyzer with pretrained deep contextual representations (Kaz-RoBERTa \cite{sagyndyk2025kazroberta}) creates a dual-stream architecture wherein symbolic linguistic priors guide neural feature extraction, neutralizing subword fragmentation and bolstering cross-generator robustness.

Second, text safety requires decoupling \textit{generative origin} from \textit{factual veracity}. Machine-generated texts can articulate verified truths, while human writing may spread rumors or disinformation. Conversely, synthetic prose can hallucinate falsehoods, and human prose can articulate verified facts. By introducing the first specialized Kazakh fact verification benchmark (\textbf{Kazakh-FEVER 3K}) and formulating a continuous 2D Cartesian Trust Matrix $(T_{\text{fact}}, T_{\text{gen}})$, this research establishes an interpretable verification paradigm equipping auditors with integrated provenance and veracity diagnostics.
