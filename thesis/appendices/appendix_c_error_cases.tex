\section{Forensic Walkthrough Across Cartesian Trust Quadrants}
\label{sec:app_error_quadrants}

To evaluate how our joint framework decouples generation origin ($T_{\text{gen}}$) from factual veracity ($T_{\text{fact}}$), we conduct deep forensic walkthroughs of six representative real-world texts spanning all four Cartesian quadrants and two boundary stress cases.

\textbf{Kazakh Original:} \textit{``Қаныш Имантайұлы Сәтбаев 1899 жылы Павлодар облысы Баянауыл ауданында туған. Ол 1946 жылы жаңадан құрылған Қазақ КСР Ғылым Академиясының тұңғыш президенті болып сайланды және Жезқазған мыс кен орнын ашқан ғалым ретінде тарихқа енді.''} \\
\textbf{English Translation:} \textit{``Kanysh Imantayuly Satbayev was born in 1899 in the Bayanaul district of Pavlodar region. \textbf{Provenance / Register:} Authentic Human Historical Essay (Literary encyclopedic prose). \\

\section{Systematic Failure Mode Taxonomy}
\label{sec:app_error_taxonomy}

Detailed analysis of the residual 17.4\% verification errors and 0.42\% detection false alarms reveals four primary failure categories:

\subsection{Morphotactic Ambiguity and Homophonous Collisions}
In isolated sentences lacking explicit syntactic heads, homophonous suffix collisions cannot always be resolved:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Possessive vs. Accusative (\textit{-ы/-і}):} In the phrase \textit{``баласы кітапты оқыды''}, \textit{бала-сы} is unequivocally 3rd-person possessive. However, in short headless fragments like \textit{``жазушы романы''}, \textit{роман-ы} can function either as possessed head (``the novel of the writer'') or as colloquial non-standard accusative (``[he read] the writer's novel'').
    \item \textbf{Verbal Negation vs. Participle (\textit{-ма/-ме}):} When attached to nominalized roots, \textit{-ма/-ме} can represent either verbal negation (\textit{кел-ме} ``do not come'') or deverbal nominalization (\textit{бөл-ме} ``room'', \textit{жарна-ма} ``advertisement''). The transducer utilizes a static nominal exception lexicon to mitigate this collision, but novel neologisms can bypass filtering.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dialectal Slang and Colloquial Contractions}
Spoken regional Kazakh, particularly in Southern and Western oblasts, features phonetic elisions and non-standard auxiliary verb contractions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Standard \textit{келе жатыр} (``is coming'') $\to$ Colloquial \textit{кеватр} / \textit{кевалыр}.
    \item Standard \textit{болып жатыр} (``is happening'') $\to$ Colloquial \textit{болватр}.
    \item Standard \textit{не істеп жатырсың} (``what are you doing'') $\to$ Colloquial \textit{нешстеватсың}.
\end{itemize}
Because these colloquial contractions are absent from standard orthographic dictionaries, our FST falls back to subword character segmentation, temporarily elevating perplexity metrics.

\subsection{Multi-Hop Coreference and Temporal Chronology}
Complex verification queries involving multi-paragraph historical biographies exhibit reasoning breakdowns:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Zero Anaphora and Implicit Pronouns:} Kazakh is a pro-drop language where subject pronouns are frequently omitted due to rich verbal agreement. When evidence is split across three consecutive sentences, tracking whether the omitted subject refers to the primary historical actor or an auxiliary agent requires deep discourse parsing.
    \item \textbf{Relative Temporal Chronology:} Understanding that an event occurred \textit{``соғыстан үш жыл бұрын''} (``three years before the war'') requires cross-referencing external calendar anchor points with implicit relative arithmetic.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Novel Commercial Code-Switching Neologisms}
Emerging e-commerce slang (e.g., \textit{кликпен}, \textit{каспипэй}, \textit{кэшбэкке}) continually introduces uncatalogued foreign stems, requiring automated dynamic lexicon expansion.

\subsection{Linguistic Interpretations of Ablation Results}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Negation Alignment Ablation:} Removing the 6-allomorph negation detector causes a catastrophic 20.1 percentage point drop on the Polarity subset (from 91.3\% down to 71.2\%). Transformers fail to attend to short 2-character negation morphemes embedded deep within complex verbal suffix chains.
    \item \textbf{Temporal/Calendar Extractor Ablation:} Eliminating the temporal normalizer reduces performance on date-discrepant claims by 19.3 percentage points (88.7\% to 69.4\%), demonstrating that standard cross-encoders treat 4-digit Gregorian years as unrelated subword tokens.
    \item \textbf{Evidentials/Modals Ablation:} Removing evidential feature detection drops performance on hearsay claims by 19.5 percentage points (86.8\% to 67.3\%), proving that morphological tracking of \textit{-ыпты/-іпті} vs. \textit{-ды/-ді} is essential for detecting nuanced AI hallucinations.
    \item \textbf{FST Root Morphemic Stemming Ablation:} Removing the FST entirely degrades performance across all six syntactic categories uniformly ($-7.3$ pp overall accuracy), confirming that morpheme-aligned tokenization is the foundational cornerstone of reliable NLP in agglutinative languages.
\end{enumerate}
