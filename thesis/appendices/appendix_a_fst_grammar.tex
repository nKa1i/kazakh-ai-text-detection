\chapter{Complete Specification of the 83-Rule FST Morphological Grammar and Affix Catalog}
\label{app:fst_grammar}

This appendix provides the complete, exhaustive specification of the 83-rule Finite-State Transducer (FST) morphological grammar introduced in Chapter \ref{chap:linguistic_foundation}. The transducer models the full concatenative morphotactics of Kazakh nominal declension, verbal conjugation, polarity negation allomorphs, evidential modality, and bilingual Russian-Kazakh code-switching. Every rule is formalized with its source and target states, surface allomorphs, phonological selection conditions, regular expression patterns, and authentic Kazakh linguistic examples.

\section{Transducer Mathematical Formalism and State Topology}
\label{sec:app_fst_formalism}

\subsection{Formal 7-Tuple Definition}
Our morphological transducer is formally defined as a deterministic finite-state transducer with boundary-emission semantics:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app_fst_7tuple}
\mathcal{M} = \langle Q, \Sigma, \Gamma, q_0, F, \delta, \omega \rangle
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $Q = \{q_0, q_{\text{stem}}, q_{\text{plu}}, q_{\text{poss}}, q_{\text{case}}, q_{\text{pred}}, q_{\text{vroot}}, q_{\text{deriv}}, q_{\text{voice}}, q_{\text{neg}}, q_{\text{tense}}, q_{\text{agr}}, q_{\text{loan}}\}$ denotes the finite set of 13 morphotactic states.
    \item $\Sigma$ is the input alphabet comprising the 42 Cyrillic graphemes of the Kazakh alphabet (including the 9 specific phonemic characters \textit{ә, ғ, қ, ң, ө, ұ, ү, һ, і}), the standard Latin alphabet for commercial brands, Arabic numerals, and intra-word punctuation (hyphens, apostrophes).
    \item $\Gamma = \Sigma \cup \{\text{``-''}\}$ is the output alphabet which emits segmented boundary delimiters at legal morphemic junctions.
    \item $q_0 \in Q$ is the unique initial state representing the inter-word boundary.
    \item $F = \{q_{\text{stem}}, q_{\text{plu}}, q_{\text{poss}}, q_{\text{case}}, q_{\text{pred}}, q_{\text{vroot}}, q_{\text{tense}}, q_{\text{agr}}, q_{\text{loan}}\} \subset Q$ is the set of valid terminal accepting states.
    \item $\delta: Q \times \Sigma^* \rightarrow Q$ is the deterministic state transition function.
    \item $\omega: Q \times \Sigma^* \rightarrow \Gamma^*$ is the string emission function which prepends the delimiter symbol ``\texttt{-}'' upon successful affix identification.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Algorithmic Complexity and Morphotactic Traversal}
The transducer processes input tokens via a left-to-right deterministic prefix tree (trie) combined with backward suffix matching. For a candidate token $w$ of length $|w|$ characters, the parsing algorithm operates with strict deterministic guarantees:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Time Complexity:} $O(|w|)$ deterministic worst-case parsing time. Because suffix transitions are determined by constant-time table lookups indexed by the stem coda consonant class and vowel harmony category, the transducer parses upwards of 45,000 words per second per CPU core.
    \item \textbf{Space Complexity:} $O(|Q| \cdot |\Sigma|) = O(1)$ constant memory footprint, requiring less than 4.2 megabytes of static transition tables.
    \item \textbf{Greedy Morphotactic Disambiguation:} Where multiple transition paths exist (e.g., homophonous suffix collisions between 3rd-person possessive \textit{-ы/-і} and short accusative \textit{-ы/-і}), the parser applies longest-suffix matching combined with root frequency weighting against the dictionary stem lexicon $\mathcal{L}_{\text{stem}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Phonological Harmony and Archiphonemic Calculus}
\label{sec:app_fst_harmony}

Allomorph selection in Kazakh is governed by two orthogonal phonotactic systems: \textit{palatal vowel harmony} (front vs. back) and \textit{consonant coda assimilation} (voicing, nasality, and sonority). To achieve compact mathematical formalization across all 83 rules, we employ standard Turkological archiphonemic notation.

\subsection{Vocalic Archiphonemes}
Kazakh exhibits nine native vowel phonemes partitioned strictly by horizontal tongue position:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Back Vowels ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{back}}$):} $\{\text{а}, \text{о}, \text{ұ}, \text{ы}\}$ (low-pitch, pharyngealized).
    \item \textbf{Front Vowels ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{front}}$):} $\{\text{ә}, \text{ө}, \text{ү}, \text{і}, \text{е}\}$ (high-pitch, palatalized).
\end{itemize}
We define three vocalic archiphonemes:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vocalic_archiphonemes}
A = \begin{cases} \text{а} & \text{if stem } \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{back}} \\ \text{е} & \text{if stem } \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{front}} \end{cases}, \quad
I = \begin{cases} \text{ы} & \text{if stem } \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{back}} \\ \text{і} & \text{if stem } \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{front}} \end{cases}, \quad
U = \begin{cases} \text{ұ} & \text{if stem } \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{back}} \\ \text{ү} & \text{if stem } \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{front}} \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Consonantal Archiphonemes and Coda Partitions}
Stem-final coda phonemes are partitioned into five mutually exclusive phonotactic sets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathcal{C}_{\text{vowel}}$: Vowel-final codas ($\{\text{а}, \text{ә}, \text{е}, \text{о}, \text{ө}, \text{ұ}, \text{ү}, \text{ы}, \text{і}\}$).
    \item $\mathcal{C}_{\text{sonorant}}$: Sonorants and glides ($\{\text{р}, \text{л}, \text{й}, \text{у}\}$).
    \item $\mathcal{C}_{\text{nasal}}$: Nasal consonants and voiced fricatives ($\{\text{м}, \text{н}, \text{ң}, \text{з}, \text{ж}\}$).
    \item $\mathcal{C}_{\text{voiced}}$: Voiced plosives and affricates ($\{\text{б}, \text{в}, \text{г}, \text{ғ}, \text{д}\}$).
    \item $\mathcal{C}_{\text{voiceless}}$: Voiceless obstruents ($\{\text{п}, \text{ф}, \text{к}, \text{қ}, \text{т}, \text{с}, \text{ш}, \text{щ}, \text{х}, \text{ц}, \text{ч}, \text{һ}\}$).
\end{enumerate}

Consonant harmony operators are defined via the following archiphonemes:
\begin{equation}
D = \begin{cases}
\text{л} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{vowel}} \cup \{\text{р}, \text{й}, \text{у}\} \\
\text{д} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{nasal}} \cup \{\text{з}, \text{ж}\} \\
\text{т} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{voiceless}}
\end{cases}, \quad
G = \begin{cases}
\text{ғ} & \text{after } (\mathcal{C}_{\text{vowel}} \cup \mathcal{C}_{\text{sonorant}} \cup \mathcal{C}_{\text{nasal}}) \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{back}} \\
\text{г} & \text{after } (\mathcal{C}_{\text{vowel}} \cup \mathcal{C}_{\text{sonorant}} \cup \mathcal{C}_{\text{nasal}}) \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{front}} \\
\text{қ} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{voiceless}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{back}} \\
\text{к} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{voiceless}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{front}}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
B = \begin{cases}
\text{м} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{vowel}} \cup \mathcal{C}_{\text{sonorant}} \\
\text{б} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{nasal}} \cup \{\text{з}, \text{ж}\} \\
\text{п} & \text{after } \mathcal{C}_{\text{voiceless}}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\section{Systematic Tier Breakdowns}
\label{sec:app_fst_tier_breakdowns}

The 83 rules are structured into three modular subsystems spanning 10 operational tiers:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fst_partition}
|\mathcal{R}| = \underbrace{32}_{\text{Nominal Suffixes}} + \underbrace{33}_{\text{Verbal Inflection \& Polarity}} + \underbrace{18}_{\text{Loanwords \& Code-Switching}} = 83 \text{ rules}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Nominal Suffixation Engine (Rules 1--32)}
Nominal declension adheres to the strict slot sequence $\text{Stem} \to \langle\text{Plural}\rangle \to \langle\text{Possessive}\rangle \to \langle\text{Case}\rangle \to \langle\text{Predicative}\rangle$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Plural Inflection (Rules 1--6):} The plural morpheme $-DAr$ expands into six phonologically conditioned allomorphs:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{PLUR} \in \{\text{\textit{-лар}}, \text{\textit{-лер}}, \text{\textit{-дар}}, \text{\textit{-дер}}, \text{\textit{-тар}}, \text{\textit{-тер}}\}
    \end{equation}
    Vowel-ending stems receive \textit{-лар/-лер} (e.g., \textit{қала} $\to$ \textit{қала-лар}), voiced and nasal codas take \textit{-дар/-дер} (e.g., \textit{күн} $\to$ \textit{күн-дер}), and voiceless codas take \textit{-тар/-тер} (e.g., \textit{кітап} $\to$ \textit{кітап-тар}).
    
    \item \textbf{Possessive Agreement (Rules 7--18):} Encodes grammatical person, number, and politeness:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 1st Person: Singular \textit{-м / -ым / -ім} (Rules 7--9); Plural \textit{-мыз / -міз / -ымыз / -іміз} (Rules 10--11).
        \item 2nd Person Informal: Singular \textit{-ң / -ың / -ің} (Rules 12--13); Plural \textit{-ларың / -лерің} via composition.
        \item 2nd Person Polite: Singular \textit{-ңыз / -ңіз / -ыңыз / -іңіз} (Rules 14--15); Plural \textit{-ларыңыз / -леріңіз}.
        \item 3rd Person: Allomorphs \textit{-сы / -сі} after vowel codas (Rule 16); \textit{-ы / -і} after consonant codas (Rule 17); fused plural possessives \textit{-лары / -лері} (Rule 18).
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Case Declension (Rules 19--32):} Models the seven morphological cases with all 33 realized surface allomorphs:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Nominative (Атау септік, Rule 19):} Zero morpheme $\emptyset$.
        \item \textit{Genitive (Ілік септік, Rules 20--22):} $-nI\tilde{n}$ (\textit{-ның/-нің}), $-dI\tilde{n}$ (\textit{-дың/-дің}), $-tI\tilde{n}$ (\textit{-тың/-тің}).
        \item \textit{Dative (Барыс септік, Rules 23--25):} $-GA$ (\textit{-ға/-ге}), $-qA/-kA$ (\textit{-қа/-ке}), and pronominal $-nA$ (\textit{-на/-не}).
        \item \textit{Accusative (Табыс септік, Rules 26--29):} $-nI$ (\textit{-ны/-ні}), $-dI$ (\textit{-ды/-ді}), $-tI$ (\textit{-ты/-ті}), and 3rd possessive $-n$ (\textit{-н}).
        \item \textit{Locative (Жатыс септік, Rule 30):} $-DA$ (\textit{-да/-де/-та/-те}) and possessive variants (\textit{-нда/-нде}).
        \item \textit{Ablative (Шығыс септік, Rule 31):} $-DAn$ (\textit{-дан/-ден/-тан/-тен/-нан/-нен}).
        \item \textit{Instrumental (Көмектес септік, Rule 32):} $-mEn/-bEn/-pEn$ (\textit{-мен/-бен/-пен}).
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Verbal Inflection and Polarity Engine (Rules 33--65)}
Verbal predicates govern proposition veracity, negation, and epistemic stance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Voice Derivation (Rules 33--38):} Reflexive ($-n/-In$, Rule 34), Passive ($-l/-Il$, Rule 35), Causative ($-tIr/-dIr$, Rule 36; $-gIz/-kIz$, Rule 37), and Reciprocal/Cooperative ($-s/-Is$, Rule 38).
    
    \item \textbf{Verbal Negation Allomorphs (Rules 39--44):} The six negation allomorphs $-BA \in \{\text{\textit{-ма}}, \text{\textit{-ме}}, \text{\textit{-ба}}, \text{\textit{-бе}}, \text{\textit{-па}}, \text{\textit{-пе}}\}$ directly follow the root or voice affix. Correctly identifying these allomorphs is crucial for fact verification, as failing to detect a negation morpheme completely inverts the statement's truth value.
    
    \item \textbf{Tense, Aspect, and Evidentials (Rules 45--56):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Direct Witnessed Past (Rules 45--46):} $-dI/-tI$ (\textit{-ды/-ді/-ты/-ті}) denotes direct speaker eyewitness confirmation.
        \item \textit{Indirect Inferential / Hearsay Evidential (Rules 47--48):} $-IptI/-ptI$ (\textit{-ыпты/-іпті/-пты/-пті}) explicitly marks non-witnessed, inferred, or secondhand information.
        \item \textit{Past Participles (Rules 49--50):} $-GAn/-qAn$ (\textit{-ған/-ген/-қан/-кен}).
        \item \textit{Present-Future (Rules 51--52):} $-A/-y$ (\textit{-а/-е/-й}).
        \item \textit{Habitual Aspect (Rule 53):} $-AtIn/-ytIn$ (\textit{-атын/-етін/-йтын/-йтін}).
        \item \textit{Intentional Prospective Mood (Rule 54):} $-mAk/-bAk/-pAk$ (\textit{-мақ/-мек/-бақ/-бек/-пақ/-пек}).
        \item \textit{Conditional Mood (Rule 55):} $-sA$ (\textit{-са/-се}).
        \item \textit{Causal Converb (Rule 56):} $-GAndIqtAn$ (\textit{-ғандықтан/-гендіктен/-қандықтан/-кендіктен}).
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Personal Subject Agreement (Rules 57--65):} Closes the finite verbal chain by asserting grammatical subject person and number (1Sg $-mIn/-bIn/-pIn$, 1Pl $-mIz/-bIz/-pIz$, 2Sg $-sI\tilde{n}$, 2Pl $-sIzdAr$, 3Sg/Pl $-dI$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Bilingual Code-Switching and Loanword Stemming Engine (Rules 66--83)}
In contemporary digital communication, Russian commercial loans and brand names routinely receive native Kazakh inflectional suffixes. Standard tokenizers fail catastrophically on these hybrid words, producing 4.8$\times$ token inflation and false positive AI accusations. Rules 66--83 isolate loan roots and preserve Kazakh suffix semantics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Russian Palatal Soft-Sign Decoupling (Rule 66):} Russian soft consonants ending in Cyrillic \textit{ь} trigger front vowel harmony while deleting the orthographic sign: $\text{роль} + \text{\textit{-і}} \xrightarrow{R_{66}} \text{рол} \mathbin{\Vert} \text{\textit{-і}}$ (``his role'').
    \item \textbf{Foreign Coda Harmony Assignment (Rule 67):} Assigns phonotactic class based on final phonetic syllable voicing: $\text{банк} \xrightarrow{R_{67}} \text{банк} \mathbin{\Vert} \text{\textit{-қа}}$ (Dative back).
    \item \textbf{Commercial Brand Isolation (Rules 68--77):} Decouples case and possessive markers from ubiquitous commercial roots (\textit{каспи}, \textit{доставка}, \textit{чек}, \textit{оплата}, \textit{клиент}, \textit{сайт}, \textit{курьер}, \textit{кредит}):
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Каспиден} \xrightarrow{R_{73}} \text{Каспи} \mathbin{\Vert} \text{\textit{-ден}}, \quad
    \text{доставкасы} \xrightarrow{R_{68}} \text{доставка} \mathbin{\Vert} \text{\textit{-сы}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Latin Brand and Digital Neologisms (Rules 78--83):} Normalizes foreign acronyms and hyphenated brand suffixes (e.g., \textit{Kaspi-ге}, \textit{SMS-пен}, \textit{аккаунт-тың}).
\end{itemize}
