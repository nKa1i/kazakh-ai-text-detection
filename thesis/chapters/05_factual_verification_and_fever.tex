\chapter{Evidence-Grounded Factual Verification and Kazakh-FEVER}
\label{chap:factual_verification}

This chapter introduces \textbf{Kazakh-FEVER 3K}, the first human-validated benchmark for automated fact verification in Kazakh, and presents our end-to-end evidence retrieval and verification architecture designed to overcome agglutinative lexical sparsity and adversarial morphological traps.

\section{Motivation: Decoupling Origin from Veracity}
\label{sec:ver_motivation}

While machine-generated text detectors can reliably determine whether a text exhibits statistical characteristics of neural generation, they are incapable of assessing whether the factual propositions asserted within that text correspond to empirical reality. An AI model can hallucinate falsehoods with perfect grammatical fluency, while human users frequently generate rumors or unverified claims. Reliable information defense requires decoupling provenance from veracity by constructing an evidence-grounded verification system capable of cross-referencing asserted claims against an authoritative knowledge base.

\section{The Kazakh-FEVER 3K Benchmark}
\label{sec:ver_benchmark}

To benchmark fact verification in Kazakh, we construct \textbf{Kazakh-FEVER 3K}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Knowledge Corpus:} A curated corpus of 250,000 clean encyclopedic passages extracted from Kazakh Wikipedia, news archives, and verified historical records.
    \item \textbf{Claim-Evidence Annotation:} A total of 3,000 balanced claim-evidence pairs curated by native Kazakh linguists following the standard FEVER annotation schema \citep{thorne2018fever}:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Supported ($N=1,000$):} Claims that are fully entailed by the retrieved evidence.
        \item \textbf{Refuted ($N=1,000$):} Claims that are directly contradicted by the retrieved evidence.
        \item \textbf{Not Enough Info (NEI) ($N=1,000$):} Claims whose truth value cannot be determined from the available corpus evidence.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inter-Annotator Agreement:} Validation across double-annotated subsets yields a Cohen's $\kappa = 0.84$, indicating exceptional annotation reliability.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Hard NEI Adversarial Protocol}
\label{sec:ver_hard_nei}

Standard fact-checking benchmarks are susceptible to superficial lexical heuristics: neural models learn to classify claims based on lexical overlap with evidence rather than semantic inference \citep{schuster2019fever}. In agglutinative languages, this issue is exacerbated by morphological affixes that subtly alter truth values without changing root tokens.

To neutralize these shortcuts, we introduce the \textsc{Hard NEI} adversarial curation protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Morphological Polarity Inversions:} Neutral claims generated by perturbing verbal negation allomorphs (\textit{-ба/-бе/-па/-пе/-ма/-ме}) or modal particles (\textit{емес}, \textit{жоқ}) relative to evidence sentences, forcing models to attend to fine-grained affixation.
    \item \textbf{Temporal and Numerical Shifts:} Altering cardinal/ordinal numbers, case-marked date expressions (\textit{1991 жылы} vs. \textit{1991 жылдан кейін}), or aspectual particles.
    \item \textbf{Evidential and Epistemic Traps:} Swapping indirect evidential affixes (\textit{-ыпты/-іпті}) indicating hearsay with direct past indicators (\textit{-ды/-ді}), testing whether models distinguish factual certainty from unconfirmed attribution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Two-Stage Hybrid Evidence Retrieval}
\label{sec:ver_retrieval}

Given a candidate claim $c$, the verification pipeline first retrieves the top-$k$ candidate evidence passages from the 250,000-document knowledge corpus. Standard sparse retrieval (BM25) fails on Kazakh due to affixal variations that cause zero lexical intersection between inflected words. Conversely, dense semantic retrievers stumble on exact named entities and dates.

We construct a hybrid retriever fusing sparse lexical search and dense neural representations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Morpheme-Stemmed BM25:} Preprocesses corpus passages and queries using our 83-rule FST stemmer, reducing all words to canonical stems and indexing them via BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25}.
    \item \textbf{Dense Multilingual Retrieval:} Encodes queries and passages into dense 768-dimensional vectors using \textsc{mContriever} \citep{izacard2021contriever}.
    \item \textbf{Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF):} Combines the separate rankings $\mathcal{R}_{\text{sparse}}$ and $\mathcal{R}_{\text{dense}}$ using RRF \citep{cormack2009reciprocal} with smoothing constant $k_0 = 60$:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{RRF\_Score}(d \in \mathcal{D}) = \frac{1}{k_0 + r_{\text{sparse}}(d)} + \frac{1}{k_0 + r_{\text{dense}}(d)}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
As shown in Table \ref{tab:retrieval_results}, our hybrid pipeline achieves an outstanding Recall@5 of 99.8\% and a Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of 0.994, eliminating retrieval bottlenecks.

\section{16-Dimensional Morphological Diagnostic Cross-Encoder}
\label{sec:ver_cross_encoder}

Given claim $c$ and retrieved evidence $e$, the verification classifier predicts the final label $\hat{y} \in \{\text{Supported}, \text{Refuted}, \text{NEI}\}$. Standard cross-encoders fine-tuned purely on text tokens frequently overlook subtle affix contradictions. 

We augment the cross-encoder with a 16-dimensional morphological diagnostic feature extractor $\bm{\psi}(c, e) \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ that explicitly computes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Negation Alignment (dims 1--3):} Polarity agreement between claim and evidence verbal predicates.
    \item \textbf{Temporal / Calendar Consistency (dims 4--6):} Match of temporal locatives and numerical dates.
    \item \textbf{Evidentials \& Modals (dims 7--9):} Compatibility of epistemic and evidential suffixes.
    \item \textbf{Entity \& Case Role Matching (dims 10--13):} Consistency of case affixes (Subject-Nom vs. Object-Acc vs. Source-Abl).
    \item \textbf{Root Morpheme Overlap (dims 14--16):} Exact lexical root intersection normalized by FST stems.
\end{itemize}
These diagnostic features are concatenated with the cross-encoder $[\text{CLS}]$ embedding and trained with class-weighted cross-entropy and cosine learning rate warmup.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:ver_summary}

This chapter demonstrated the necessity of linguistic grounding for fact verification in agglutinative languages. Through hybrid RRF retrieval and 16-dimensional morphological diagnostic reasoning, our system effectively neutralizes lexical shortcuts on Kazakh-FEVER 3K. In Chapter \ref{chap:system_explainability}, we synthesize detection and verification into a continuous 2D Trust Matrix and interactive production dashboard.
