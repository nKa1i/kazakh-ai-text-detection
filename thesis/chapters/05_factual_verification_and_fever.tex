\chapter{Evidence-Grounded Factual Verification and Kazakh-FEVER}
\label{chap:factual_verification}

In the preceding chapter, we established a generator-invariant detection framework capable of determining whether text exhibits statistical characteristics of artificial generation. However, in open digital communication, provenance and veracity are fundamentally orthogonal: an authentic human-written post may propagate dangerous health disinformation or fraudulent reviews, whereas an AI language model may synthesize an impeccably sourced historical summary. Automated content defense in high-stakes environments requires decoupling stylistic provenance from empirical truth.

This chapter presents the second core pillar of our dissertation: an end-to-end evidence-grounded factual verification framework designed specifically for morphologically complex and agglutinative languages. First, we formalize the \textbf{Decoupling Principle}, establishing the theoretical necessity of joint veracity and provenance auditing. Second, we introduce \textbf{Kazakh-FEVER 3K}, the first human-validated benchmark for automated claim verification in Kazakh, and establish the adversarial \textbf{Hard NEI} protocol. Third, we design a \textbf{Two-Stage Hybrid Evidence Retrieval Architecture} fusing finite-state morpheme-stemmed BM25 with dense multilingual representations via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), eliminating agglutinative lexical sparsity. Fourth, we formulate a \textbf{16-Dimensional Morphological Diagnostic Cross-Encoder} that extracts fine-grained polarity, temporal, evidential, and morphemic agreement features, trained under a class-weighted loss and differential optimization schedule. Finally, we report comprehensive empirical evaluations and ablation studies on the 250,000-passage Kazakh knowledge corpus and Kazakh-FEVER test suite.

\section{The Decoupling Principle: Orthogonality of Style and Truth}
\label{sec:decoupling_principle}

A prevalent misconception in early AI safety literature was the conflation of generative text detection with truth auditing \citep{sadasivan2023can,wang2024raid}. In reality, autoregressive foundation models are optimized for linguistic probability and surface coherence rather than empirical correspondence with reality. 

\subsection{Theoretical Orthogonality}
Let $\mathcal{X}$ denote the space of natural language texts. For any text $x \in \mathcal{X}$, we define two independent latent variables:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generative Provenance ($Y_{\text{gen}} \in \{0, 1\}$):} Where $Y_{\text{gen}} = 0$ indicates authentic human authorship, and $Y_{\text{gen}} = 1$ indicates synthetic generation by an artificial language model.
    \item \textbf{Factual Veracity ($Y_{\text{fact}} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$):} Relative to an authoritative external knowledge corpus $\mathcal{E}$, where $Y_{\text{fact}} = +1$ denotes empirical truth (\textsc{Supported}), $Y_{\text{fact}} = -1$ denotes factual contradiction (\textsc{Refuted}), and $Y_{\text{fact}} = 0$ denotes epistemic ambiguity or absence of corroborating evidence (\textsc{Not Enough Info}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Epistemic Orthogonality]
The conditional probability distribution of factual truth is conditionally independent of generative provenance given the world knowledge base $\mathcal{E}$:
\begin{equation}
    P(Y_{\text{fact}} \mid x, Y_{\text{gen}}, \mathcal{E}) = P(Y_{\text{fact}} \mid x, \mathcal{E})
\end{equation}
Conversely, the probability that text $x$ is machine-generated depends on stylistic and statistical token transitions, independent of its factual validity:
\begin{equation}
    P(Y_{\text{gen}} \mid x, Y_{\text{fact}}) = P(Y_{\text{gen}} \mid x)
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Practical Consequences for Content Forensics}
The independence of $Y_{\text{gen}}$ and $Y_{\text{fact}}$ induces four distinct operational scenarios in digital media:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Human Truth ($Y_{\text{gen}}=0, Y_{\text{fact}}=+1$):} High-credibility human journalism, academic scholarship, and authentic witness testimonies.
    \item \textbf{Human Disinformation ($Y_{\text{gen}}=0, Y_{\text{fact}}=-1$):} Rumors, political propaganda, and fraudulent human consumer feedback. A pure AI detector would classify these texts as benign (human-written), failing to flag the falsehood.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Hallucination ($Y_{\text{gen}}=1, Y_{\text{fact}}=-1$):} Generative LLMs hallucinating non-existent historical events or erroneous medical dosages with polished grammatical fluency.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Truth ($Y_{\text{gen}}=1, Y_{\text{fact}}=+1$):} Benign automated summarization, educational question-answering, and machine translations grounded in verified sources. Penalizing these texts purely because they were synthesized by an LLM is unjustifiable.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, defending information ecosystems against misinformation requires evaluating assertions against empirical evidence extracted from a knowledge base.

\section{The Kazakh-FEVER 3K Benchmark}
\label{sec:fever_benchmark}

Automated fact-checking has been extensively researched for English through benchmarks like FEVER \citep{thorne2018fever} and FEVEROUS \citep{aly2021feverous}, and for high-resource question answering \citep{clark2020tydi}. However, prior to our work, zero evidence-grounded fact-checking benchmarks existed for Kazakh or any other Kipchak Turkic language.

To fill this empirical void, we construct \textbf{Kazakh-FEVER 3K}, a human-annotated, evidence-grounded verification benchmark designed specifically for agglutinative language properties \citep{anekesh2026kazakhfever}.

\subsection{Knowledge Corpus Construction}
The reference knowledge repository $\mathcal{E}$ was assembled by harvesting, cleaning, and partitioning 250,000 encyclopedic passage chunks from Kazakh Wikipedia, verified archives, and national digital libraries:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Passage Normalization:} Text chunks were normalized for Kazakh Cyrillic orthography, stripping HTML markup and administrative metadata while preserving punctuation, numerals, and capitalization.
    \item \textbf{Syntactic Completeness:} Each passage chunk was segmented into complete sentence units spanning 60 to 120 words (averaging 3.8 sentences per chunk), ensuring full context for evidential reasoning.
    \item \textbf{Corpus Indexing:} All 250,000 passages were indexed across both inverted sparse and dense vector representations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Annotation Protocol and Quality Control}
The claim-evidence dataset was authored and validated by native Kazakh linguists and professional journalists following a rigorous three-stage protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Claim Authoring:} Given an authoritative reference passage, annotators extracted verifiable factual propositions and generated candidate claims across three mutually exclusive classes:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Supported ($N=1,000$):} Claims that are fully entailed by the evidence sentences.
        \item \textbf{Refuted ($N=1,000$):} Claims that are explicitly contradicted by the evidence sentences.
        \item \textbf{Not Enough Info (NEI) ($N=1,000$):} Claims that make plausible factual assertions whose truth value cannot be corroborated or refuted from the corpus.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gold Evidence Selection:} Annotators highlighted the minimal, sufficient set of sentences in $\mathcal{E}$ required to substantiate or refute the claim.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Annotator Agreement:} A randomly sampled 20\% multi-annotator subset ($N=600$) was independently evaluated by secondary annotators. Inter-annotator agreement yielded a \textbf{Cohen's Kappa of $\kappa = 0.84$} and a \textbf{Fleiss' Kappa of $0.81$}, establishing exceptional labeling consistency.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dataset Stratification and Thematic Domains}
The benchmark spans eight distinct thematic domains: \textit{history}, \textit{geography}, \textit{literature}, \textit{law}, \textit{society}, \textit{arts}, \textit{technology}, and \textit{natural science}. Table \ref{tab:dataset_stats} details the dataset partition structure.

\section{The Hard NEI Adversarial Curation Protocol}
\label{sec:hard_nei_protocol}

In standard English fact verification benchmarks \citep{thorne2018fever}, neural models frequently exploit superficial lexical heuristics rather than performing semantic inference \citep{schuster2019fever}. Because \textsc{Not Enough Info} instances are typically generated by pairing claims with unrelated passages, deep models learn a naive shortcut: high lexical overlap predicts \textsc{Supported}, while low lexical overlap predicts \textsc{NEI}.

In agglutinative languages, this vulnerability is substantially more hazardous: grammatical affixes alter semantic polarity, chronological scope, or epistemic certainty without altering lexical root tokens. A model relying on surface subword overlap will fail catastrophically when an adversarial claim shares 90\% of its root words with an evidence passage but subtly contradicts its grammatical affixes.

To neutralize these shortcuts, we formulated the \textsc{Hard NEI} adversarial curation protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Morphological Polarity Inversions:} Annotators construct adversarial claims by modifying verbal negation allomorphs (\textit{-ба/-бе/-па/-пе/-ма/-ме}), negative past participles (\textit{-маған/-меген}), aspectual negations (\textit{-майды/-мейді}), or copular particles (\textit{емес}, \textit{жоқ}). For example:
    \begin{quote}
    \textbf{Evidence:} \textit{Абай Құнанбаев 1845 жылы Семей өңірінде дүниеге келді.} (Abai Kunanbayev was born in 1845 in the Semey region.) \\
    \textbf{Hard NEI Claim:} \textit{Абай Құнанбаев 1845 жылы Семей өңірінде дүниеге келмеген еді, ол кейін көшіп келді.} (Abai Kunanbayev was not born in Semey in 1845; he moved there later.)
    \end{quote}
    Here, lexical root overlap is 85.7\%, yet the proposition is ungrounded and contradicted.
    
    \item \textbf{Temporal and Calendar Shifts:} Modifying 4-digit calendar years, ordinal numerals, or case-marked temporal expressions (e.g., changing \textit{1991 жылы} [in 1991] to \textit{1991 жылдан кейін} [after 1991] or \textit{1993 жылы}).
    
    \item \textbf{Evidential and Epistemic Traps:} Swapping direct witnessed past suffixes (\textit{-ды/-ді}) for indirect inferential hearsay markers (\textit{-ыпты/-іпті}, \textit{екен}) or modal operators (\textit{мүмкін}, \textit{тиіс}), testing whether models distinguish confirmed historical fact from unverified rumor or hypothesis.
    
    \item \textbf{Quantifier and Scope Shifts:} Replacing universal quantifiers (\textit{барлық}, \textit{барша}---all) with partial or existential quantifiers (\textit{кейбір}, \textit{кейде}---some, occasionally).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Two-Stage Hybrid Evidence Retrieval}
\label{sec:hybrid_retrieval}

Given candidate claim $c$, the verification pipeline must retrieve the minimal set of corroborating evidence passages $E = \{e_1, \dots, e_k\}$ from the 250,000-document knowledge corpus $\mathcal{E}$.

\subsection{The Agglutinative Lexical Sparsity Problem}
Standard information retrieval relies on surface term frequency algorithms like BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25}. In Kazakh, nominal and verbal stems take dozens of inflectional allomorphs. For instance, the entity \textit{Абай} (Abai) surfaces as \textit{Абайдың} (Genitive), \textit{Абайға} (Dative), \textit{Абайды} (Accusative), \textit{Абайда} (Locative), \textit{Абайдан} (Ablative), or \textit{Абаймен} (Instrumental). In an inverted index built on unstemmed surface words, a query containing \textit{Абайға} has zero term overlap with a passage containing \textit{Абайдың}, causing BM25 to drop relevant passages.

Conversely, multilingual dense retrievers like \textsc{mContriever} \citep{izacard2021contriever} capture latent semantic topics but struggle with exact entity matches, low-resource proper nouns, and numerical dates.

\subsection{Retrieval Fusion Architecture}
To resolve this trade-off, we construct a hybrid retrieval architecture combining finite-state morpheme-stemmed sparse search with multilingual dense representation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Morpheme-Stemmed BM25:} Every query claim $c$ and knowledge passage $e \in \mathcal{E}$ is parsed by our 83-rule FST stemmer (Chapter \ref{chap:linguistic_foundation}), stripping inflectional and possessive suffixes while preserving canonical lexical stems, yielding stemmed representations $c_{\text{morph}}$ and $e_{\text{morph}}$. The BM25 score is computed as:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:bm25_stemmed}
        S_{\text{BM25}}(c_{\text{morph}}, e_{\text{morph}}) = \sum_{t \in c_{\text{morph}}} \mathrm{IDF}(t) \cdot \frac{f(t, e_{\text{morph}}) \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{f(t, e_{\text{morph}}) + k_1 \left(1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|e_{\text{morph}}|}{\mathrm{avgdl}}\right)}
    \end{equation}
    where $k_1 = 1.5$, $b = 0.75$, and $\mathrm{avgdl}$ is the average document length across $\mathcal{E}$.
    
    \item \textbf{Dense Semantic Retrieval (\textsc{mContriever}):} Concurrently, surface strings $c$ and $e$ are encoded into 768-dimensional dense vectors using pretrained multilingual contrastive encoders \citep{izacard2021contriever}:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{h}_c = \mathrm{Encoder}_{\text{dense}}(c), \quad \mathbf{h}_e = \mathrm{Encoder}_{\text{dense}}(e) \in \mathbb{R}^{768}
    \end{equation}
    yielding dense cosine similarity $S_{\text{dense}}(c, e) = \cos(\mathbf{h}_c, \mathbf{h}_e)$.
    
    \item \textbf{Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF):} To merge the sparse and dense rank lists without requiring fragile score calibration, we synthesize candidates via Reciprocal Rank Fusion \citep{cormack2009reciprocal,karpukhin2020dpr}:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:rrf_fusion}
        \mathrm{RRF}(c, e) = \frac{1}{k_0 + r_{\text{sparse}}(c, e)} + \frac{1}{k_0 + r_{\text{dense}}(c, e)}
    \end{equation}
    where $r_{\text{sparse}}$ and $r_{\text{dense}}$ are the integer rank indices of passage $e$, and $k_0 = 60$ is a smoothing constant.
\end{enumerate}

\section[16-Dimensional Morphological Cross-Encoder]{16-Dimensional Morphological Diagnostic Cross-Encoder}
\label{sec:morpho_cross_encoder}

Given candidate claim $c$ and retrieved evidence passage $E = \{e_1, \dots, e_k\}$, the verification model must predict the categorical veracity label:
\begin{equation}
    y \in \{\textsc{Supported}, \textsc{Refuted}, \textsc{Not Enough Info}\}
\end{equation}

Standard neural cross-encoders feed the concatenated sequence $\texttt{[CLS]} \circ c \circ \texttt{[SEP]} \circ E \circ \texttt{[SEP]}$ into a pretrained transformer backbone to extract the pooled contextual representation $\mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]} \in \mathbb{R}^d$. When claims share high surface token overlap with evidence but differ in fine-grained grammatical inflections, self-attention layers alone frequently miss the morphological contradiction.

To insulate the verifier against lexical overlap traps, we extract an explicit \textbf{16-Dimensional Morphological Diagnostic Vector} $\mathbf{m}_{\text{affix}} \in [0, 1]^{16}$.

\subsection{The 16 Morphological Diagnostic Features}
The vector $\mathbf{m}_{\text{affix}} = [m_0, m_1, \dots, m_{15}]^\top$ organizes linguistic signals across five functional tiers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1: Polarity and Verbal Negation Alignment ($m_0, m_1, m_2$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $m_0 = \mathbb{I}(c \text{ has negation})$: Detects verbal negation allomorphs (\textit{-ба/-бе/-па/-пе/-ма/-ме}), negated participles (\textit{-маған/-меген}), present/future negations (\textit{-майды/-мейді}), and copula particles (\textit{емес}, \textit{жоқ}) in claim $c$.
        \item $m_1 = \mathbb{I}(E \text{ has negation})$: Detects the identical negation inventory in evidence passage $E$.
        \item $m_2 = \Delta_{\text{neg}} = |m_0 - m_1|$: The directional negation mismatch indicator (XOR). When topical overlap between claim and evidence is high but $\Delta_{\text{neg}} = 1$, this feature acts as an explicit contradiction trigger, preventing false \textsc{Supported} predictions.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Tier 2: Temporal and Numerical Conflict Detection ($m_3, m_4$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $m_3 = \Delta_{\text{year}} \in \{0, 1\}$: Calendar year mismatch. Triggers ($m_3 = 1$) when the set of 4-digit calendar years extracted from $c$ ($\mathcal{Y}_c$) and from $E$ ($\mathcal{Y}_e$) are non-empty and disjoint ($\mathcal{Y}_c \cap \mathcal{Y}_e = \emptyset$).
        \item $m_4 = \Delta_{\text{num}} \in \{0, 1\}$: Quantitative quantity mismatch. Triggers when numerical quantities asserted in $c$ are entirely absent from $E$.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Tier 3: Evidentiality and Epistemic Modality ($m_5, m_6, m_7, m_8$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $m_5, m_6 \in \{0, 1\}$: Detects indirect inferential evidential suffixes (\textit{-ыпты/-іпті}, \textit{екен}) in $c$ and $E$ respectively, separating hearsay assertions from witnessed past markers (\textit{-ды/-ді}).
        \item $m_7 \in \{0, 1\}$: Modal necessity operators (\textit{керек, тиіс, қажет, міндетті}---must, should).
        \item $m_8 \in \{0, 1\}$: Modal possibility operators (\textit{мүмкін, ықтимал}---maybe, possible), signaling hypothetical claims that warrant a \textsc{Not Enough Info} stance.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Tier 4: Morphological Root vs. Surface Overlap ($m_9, m_{10}, m_{11}$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $m_9 = J_{\text{root}} = \frac{|\mathcal{R}_c \cap \mathcal{R}_e|}{|\mathcal{R}_c \cup \mathcal{R}_e|}$: Jaccard similarity across canonical dictionary roots extracted by our 83-rule FST analyzer.
        \item $m_{10} = J_{\text{surf}} = \frac{|\mathcal{S}_c \cap \mathcal{S}_e|}{|\mathcal{S}_c \cup \mathcal{S}_e|}$: Jaccard similarity across unstemmed surface word tokens.
        \item $m_{11} = \Delta_{\text{div}} = |J_{\text{surf}} - J_{\text{root}}|$: Morphological divergence gap. A high $\Delta_{\text{div}}$ indicates that agglutinative suffixation is masking shared lexical roots.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Tier 5: Syntactic Agreement and Length Scaling ($m_{12}, m_{13}, m_{14}, m_{15}$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $m_{12} = s_{\text{match}} \in \{0, 1\}$: Indicates whether the primary grammatical subject proper noun identified in $c$ is grounded in $E$.
        \item $m_{13} = J_{\text{case}}$: Jaccard root agreement specifically across words bearing nominal case inflections (genitive, dative, accusative, locative, ablative).
        \item $m_{14} = \min(1.0, |c| / 30)$: Normalized claim token length.
        \item $m_{15} = \min(1.0, |E| / 100)$: Normalized evidence token length.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Fused Stance Classification and Training Objectives}
The contextual sequence embedding $\mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and the morphological diagnostic vector $\mathbf{m}_{\text{affix}} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ are concatenated and projected to stance posterior probabilities:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:nli_projection}
    P(y \mid c, E) = \mathrm{softmax}\left(\mathbf{W}_v [\mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]} \mathbin{;} \mathbf{m}_{\text{affix}}] + \mathbf{b}_v\right) \in \mathbb{R}^3
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times (d + 16)}$ and $\mathbf{b}_v \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

\paragraph{Class-Weighted Loss Function:}
In the Kazakh-FEVER training split ($N=638$), class frequencies are moderately imbalanced: $N_{\textsc{Sup}} = 274$, $N_{\textsc{Ref}} = 154$, and $N_{\textsc{NEI}} = 210$. To prevent majority-class collapse onto \textsc{Supported} and penalize false certainty on adversarial \textsc{Hard NEI}, we optimize a normalized class-weighted cross-entropy loss:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:class_weighted_loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLI}} = - \sum_{k=1}^3 w_k \, y_k \log P(y = k \mid c, E)
\end{equation}
where $y_k \in \{0, 1\}$ is the one-hot ground-truth label, and class weights $w_k$ are inversely proportional to training frequency:
\begin{equation}
    w_k = 3 \cdot \frac{N_k^{-1}}{\sum_{j=1}^3 N_j^{-1}}
\end{equation}
normalized such that $\sum_{k=1}^3 w_k = 3.0$. For our training distribution, this yields exact weights:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:exact_class_weights}
    w_{\textsc{Sup}} = 0.735, \quad w_{\textsc{Ref}} = 1.307, \quad w_{\textsc{NEI}} = 0.958
\end{equation}
Assigning $w_{\textsc{Ref}} = 1.307$ forces the network to penalize missed refutations heavily, while $w_{\textsc{NEI}} = 0.958$ enforces calibrated epistemic restraint on ambiguous evidence.

\paragraph{Differential Optimization with Cosine Warmup:}
To preserve pretrained multilingual representations in the transformer backbone while allowing the newly initialized 16-D projection head to calibrate rapidly, we optimize parameters using differential learning rates:
\begin{equation}
    \text{lr}_{\text{encoder}} = 2 \times 10^{-5}, \quad \text{lr}_{\text{head}} = 2 \times 10^{-4}
\end{equation}
assigning a $10\times$ higher learning rate to the fusion layer. Optimization is conducted via AdamW ($\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$, weight decay $\lambda = 0.01$) under a cosine annealing scheduler with 10\% linear warmup across 3 epochs.

\paragraph{Linguistic Interpretation of Ablation Dynamics:}
The ablation results in Table \ref{tab:ablation_results} reveal three fundamental insights:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Primacy of FST Root Extraction:} Disabling FST root canonicalization (\textit{w/o FST Root Analysis}) causes the largest individual drop, reducing accuracy by 7.3 percentage points (from 82.6\% down to 75.3\%) and Strict Joint FEVER by 8.2 percentage points (from 71.4\% to 63.2\%). The model reverts to shallow lexical heuristics, proving that morphological grounding is essential for robust factual verification in agglutinative languages.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:ch5_summary}

In this chapter, we addressed the critical challenge of evidence-grounded factual verification for agglutinative languages. We formalized the Decoupling Principle, demonstrating the epistemic orthogonality of generative style and factual truth. We constructed Kazakh-FEVER 3K, the first human-validated verification benchmark for Kazakh, featuring 250,000 knowledge passages, 3,000 claim-evidence pairs, and the adversarial Hard NEI protocol. To eliminate lexical sparsity, we designed a two-stage hybrid retriever combining FST-stemmed BM25 with dense \textsc{mContriever} embeddings via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, achieving 99.8\% Recall@5. Finally, we developed a 16-dimensional morphological diagnostic cross-encoder trained under a class-weighted loss and differential optimization schedule, establishing state-of-the-art results (82.6\% Accuracy, 71.4\% Strict Joint FEVER, and 72.8\% Hard NEI F1).

Having established robust modules for generative text detection (Chapter \ref{chap:morpho_contrastive_detection}) and factual verification (Chapter \ref{chap:factual_verification}), Chapter \ref{chap:system_explainability} synthesizes both capabilities into a continuous 2D Cartesian Trust Matrix and an interactive production dashboard.
