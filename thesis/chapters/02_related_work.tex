\chapter{Literature Review and Related Work}
\label{chap:related_work}

This chapter establishes the theoretical, empirical, and computational foundation of this dissertation by conducting an exhaustive review across four intersecting fields of research: paradigms for detecting machine-generated text, computational morphology and NLP for Turkic languages, automated fact verification and evidence retrieval systems, and contrastive representation learning for out-of-distribution robustness. We conclude with a comprehensive comparative taxonomy contrasting existing methodologies against our proposed framework, highlighting the critical voids this dissertation resolves.

\section{Taxonomy of AI-Generated Text Detection}
\label{sec:rel_mgt_detection}

Automated mechanisms for auditing text generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) fall into three primary paradigms: zero-shot statistical estimators, generation-phase watermarking protocols, and supervised neural classifiers \citep{solaiman2019release,mitchell2023detectgpt,wang2024raid}.

\subsection{Statistical and Zero-Shot Detectors}
Zero-shot statistical detectors operate on the premise that autoregressive language models exhibit characteristic distributional signatures in their next-token probability landscapes that diverge from human writing. 

\paragraph{Token Rank Distributions}
The Giant Language Model Test Room (GLTR) \citep{gehrmann2019gltr} visualizes and measures token predictive distributions. When decoding, language models draw disproportionately from the top-$k$ positions of their softmax vocabulary head (e.g., $k \in \{10, 100, 1000\}$). Human writers, by contrast, frequently incorporate unexpected lexical selections, resulting in fat-tailed rank histograms. While GLTR provides intuitive visual analytics, setting crisp decision thresholds across diverse genres remains challenging.

\paragraph{Log-Probability Curvature and DetectGPT}
\citet{mitchell2023detectgpt} formalized the hypothesis of negative log-probability curvature: synthetic texts tend to occupy local maxima of the generating model's log-probability function. Given a candidate text $x$, DetectGPT applies a generic mask-filling model (e.g., T5) to generate $m$ local perturbations $\tilde{x}_1, \dots, \tilde{x}_m$. It then evaluates the curvature perturbation discrepancy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:detectgpt_score}
\mathbf{d}(x, p_\theta) = \log p_\theta(x) - \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^m \log p_\theta(\tilde{x}_i)
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf{d}(x, p_\theta) > \tau$, the sample is classified as machine-generated. 

\paragraph{Conditional Curvature and Perplexity Ratios}
To alleviate the extreme computational overhead of perturbation sampling in DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT \citep{bao2024fastdetectgpt} analyzes conditional probability curvature directly via cross-entropy evaluation under a reference model, achieving up to 340$\times$ speedups. In parallel, Binoculars \citep{hans2024binoculars} introduced a zero-shot thresholding metric based on the ratio between perplexity and cross-perplexity across two pre-existing foundation models ($M_1, M_2$):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binoculars_score}
B(x) = \frac{\log \mathrm{PPL}_{M_1}(x)}{\log \mathrm{X}\text{-}\mathrm{PPL}_{M_1, M_2}(x)}
\end{equation}
Binoculars achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot detection on English benchmarks without requiring fine-tuning.

\paragraph{Fundamental Limitations of Statistical Approaches}
Despite their theoretical appeal, zero-shot statistical detectors face three fatal roadblocks in real-world forensic deployment:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{White-Box Dependency:} They strictly require access to token log-probabilities $p_\theta(x)$ or matching proxy models. For closed commercial models (such as GPT-4o) or proprietary localized APIs, token probabilities are withheld or distorted by sampling.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Low-Resource Reference Models:} In low-resource languages such as Kazakh, there are no authoritative pairs of massive base and instruct foundation models with matched tokenizers to compute cross-perplexities reliably.
    \item \textbf{Failure on Short Texts:} Curvature and perplexity estimators exhibit immense variance on short texts ($\le 60$ characters), where token counts are insufficient to overcome stochastic noise.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Generation-Phase Watermarking Protocols}
To establish cryptographic provenance at source, generation-phase watermarking algorithms embed imperceptible statistical biases during decoding \citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark}. 

\paragraph{Logit Biasing and Green-Red Lists}
The canonical algorithm developed by \citet{kirchenbauer2023watermark} uses a pseudorandom hash of the previous token $t_{i-1}$ to partition the vocabulary $V$ into a ``green list'' $G_{t_{i-1}}$ of size $\gamma |V|$ (typically $\gamma = 0.5$) and a ``red list'' $R_{t_{i-1}}$. During autoregressive sampling, a positive bias $\delta > 0$ is added to the logits of green tokens:
\begin{equation}
l_k^{(i)} \leftarrow \begin{cases}
l_k^{(i)} + \delta, & \text{if } v_k \in G_{t_{i-1}} \\
l_k^{(i)}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A text of length $N$ is audited by calculating the one-sided $z$-statistic over the number of observed green tokens $|x|_G$:
\begin{equation}
z = \frac{|x|_G - \gamma N}{\sqrt{N \gamma (1 - \gamma)}}
\end{equation}
If $z$ exceeds a significance threshold (e.g., $z > 4.0$), human authorship is rejected with provably bounded false positive probability.

\paragraph{Operational Constraints of Watermarking}
While watermarking provides elegant mathematical guarantees, it cannot serve as an open-world forensic defense:
\begin{itemize}
    \item It requires universal compliance from model vendors. Rogue actors, open-weights foundation models (e.g., LLaMA, Qwen, DeepSeek), and offline local inference engines do not embed watermarks.
    \item Watermarks are susceptible to post-processing degradation, including cross-lingual translation, machine paraphrasing, and token substitution attacks \citep{sadasivan2023can,chakraborty2023possibilities}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Supervised Neural Classifiers and Generalization Vulnerabilities}
Supervised fine-tuning of deep bidirectional pretrained transformers remains the most widely deployed detection paradigm in practical content moderation \citep{solaiman2019release,uchendu2020authorship,ippolito2020automatic}. 

Typically, a pretrained encoder (e.g., RoBERTa \citep{liu2019roberta} or XLM-RoBERTa \citep{conneau2020xlmr}) is equipped with a sequence classification head and fine-tuned via cross-entropy loss over balanced corpora of human- and machine-authored documents:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = - y \log \hat{y} - (1 - y) \log (1 - \hat{y})
\end{equation}
While supervised classifiers achieve outstanding in-distribution accuracy ($\ge 98\%$), extensive recent empirical investigations have exposed alarming vulnerabilities:

\paragraph{Out-of-Distribution and Cross-Generator Degradation}
The massive RAID benchmark \citep{wang2024raid}, comprising over 6 million generations across multiple model families, and the GenAIDetect evaluation \citep{genaidetection2025} revealed that supervised neural detectors severely overfit to idiosyncratic generator-specific stylistic artifacts. A detector trained on GPT-3.5 outputs suffers an AUROC drop of 30--50 percentage points when evaluated on outputs from LLaMA-3 \citep{touvron2023llama} or Qwen-2.5 \citep{qwen2024}.

\paragraph{Demographic and Non-Native Bias}
In a seminal study, \citet{liang2023gptdetectors} demonstrated that commercial AI detectors systematically misclassify essays authored by non-native English speakers as machine-generated, exhibiting false positive rates exceeding 61\%. Because non-native writers employ more constrained, predictable vocabularies with lower perplexity, standard detectors mistake simple syntactic patterns for machine generation. 

In this dissertation, we establish that an identical and even more severe pathology afflicts agglutinative low-resource languages: standard tokenizers shatter native morphology into irregular fragments, prompting neural classifiers to flag authentic colloquial human prose as synthetic.

\section{Computational Morphology and NLP for Turkic Languages}
\label{sec:rel_turkic_morphology}

Turkic languages, encompassing Kazakh, Turkish, Uzbek, Kyrgyz, Uyghur, Tatar, and Bashkir, share a strict agglutinative morphological architecture characterized by concatenative suffixation and phonological harmony \citep{oflazer1994twolevel,tyers2016finite,bekmanova2021kazakh}.

\subsection{Two-Level Morphology and Finite-State Foundations}
The foundational computational framework for agglutinative morphology is Two-Level Morphology, formulated by \citet{koskenniemi1983twolevel}. Koskenniemi demonstrated that complex morphophonological alternations can be formalized as parallel finite-state transducers operating between two distinct representations: an abstract lexical level and a concrete surface realization.

Building on this theory, \citet{oflazer1994twolevel} developed a landmark two-level morphological analyzer for Turkish. Oflazer demonstrated that because Turkic root stems can theoretically undergo unbounded recursive affixation (e.g., causative and passive morphemes stacking iteratively), dictionary-lookup paradigms fail. Finite-state transducers (FSTs) resolve this by encoding valid morphotactic transitions as deterministic state machines, ensuring that only grammatically legal affix sequences are parsed.

For the Kipchak Turkic subgroup, \citet{tyers2016finite} established open-source finite-state morphological transducers for Kazakh, Tatar, and Kumyk within the Apertium platform using the Helsinki Finite-State Transducer (HFST) toolkit. Their work codified fundamental Kipchak morphophonological rules, providing a rigorous benchmark for rule-based grammatical processing.

\subsection{Kazakh Linguistic Resources and Corpus Linguistics}
Over the past decade, Kazakh computational linguistics has transitioned from rule-based lexicons to data-driven statistical processing:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \citet{makhambetov2013kazakh,makhambetov2015data} constructed the first large-scale annotated Kazakh language corpora and evaluated statistical morphological disambiguation and stemming. Their work established the empirical baseline morphological rate for standard literary Kazakh ($\mu^* \approx 2.202$ morphemes per word).
    \item \citet{bekmanova2021kazakh} developed a hybrid rule-based segmentation model coupling stem dictionaries with semantic categorization to enhance Kazakh text processing and information retrieval.
    \item \citet{yeshpanov2024kazsandra} introduced KazSAnDRA, a multi-domain consumer sentiment dataset scraped from Kazakhstan's leading commercial ecosystem (Kaspi). KazSAnDRA demonstrated that real-world colloquial digital text diverges sharply from formal literary prose, featuring intense Russian-Kazakh bilingual code-switching and non-standard orthography.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Neural Language Models and Subword Tokenization Pathology}
With the deep learning revolution, multilingual masked language models, such as mBERT \citep{devlin2019bert} and XLM-RoBERTa \citep{conneau2020xlmr}, became the default backbones for cross-lingual transfer. Concurrently, regional researchers developed monolingual pretrained transformers: KazBERT \citep{eraly2021kazbert} and KazRoBERTa \citep{sagyndyk2025kazroberta}.

However, all these neural models rely fundamentally on unsupervised statistical subword tokenizers, primarily Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) \citep{sennrich2016subword} or SentencePiece Unigram \citep{kudo2018sentencepiece}. These tokenizers are trained on raw byte frequencies without any morphological awareness. In agglutinative languages, this induces \textit{subword token inflation}: legal affixes are shattered into arbitrary subwords, destroying lexical root boundaries, diluting attention gradients, and severely compromising detection accuracy. Prior work has lacked an FST-grounded regularization mechanism to reconcile neural language models with agglutinative morphotactics.

\section{Automated Fact Verification and Retrieval Systems}
\label{sec:rel_fact_verification}

Automated fact-checking has evolved into a cornerstone of AI integrity, driven by standardized benchmark datasets, dense retrieval architectures, and natural language inference (NLI).

\subsection{Benchmark Evolution: FEVER, FEVEROUS, and SciFact}
The modern era of automated fact verification began with the Fact Extraction and VERification (FEVER) benchmark \citep{thorne2018fever}. FEVER formalized verification as a multi-stage task: given an asserted claim $c$, an automated system must:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Retrieve relevant evidence sentences $E^*$ from a reference corpus (Wikipedia).
    \item Predict an epistemic stance label $y \in \{\textsc{Supports}, \textsc{Refutes}, \textsc{Not Enough Info (NEI)}\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Evaluation on FEVER enforces a strict criterion: a claim is counted as correct under the \textit{Strict FEVER Score} if and only if both the predicted label matches the gold label and the retrieved evidence sentences match the annotated gold evidence set.

Subsequent benchmarks expanded this formulation: FEVEROUS \citep{aly2021feverous} introduced verification over structured tables and unstructured text, while SciFact \citep{wadden2020fact} evaluated scientific claims against expert research abstracts. However, these benchmarks were constructed exclusively in English. Non-English and agglutinative languages have lacked human-validated fact verification benchmarks.

\subsection{Lexical Overlap Shortcuts and Adversarial Protocols}
A pervasive challenge in fact verification is the susceptibility of deep classifiers to superficial dataset shortcuts. In a critical analysis, \citet{schuster2019fever} demonstrated that models trained on standard FEVER exploit spurious correlations in claim syntax: the presence of explicit negation words (e.g., ``never'', ``failed'') strongly correlates with \textsc{Refutes}, while high unigram lexical overlap between claim and evidence strongly predicts \textsc{Supports}. Consequently, models frequently achieve high accuracy on standard test sets while completely ignoring the retrieved evidence.

In agglutinative languages, this vulnerability is exceptionally dangerous. Modifying a single verbal negation allomorph (e.g., \textit{келді} ``arrived'' vs. \textit{келмеді} ``did not arrive'') completely inverts the truth value of a proposition while preserving over 90\% of character and subword overlap. Neutral claims that share identical surface roots with evidence sentences trap standard cross-encoders into confident misclassifications. Robust verification in agglutinative languages thus strictly requires adversarial benchmark design (such as our \textsc{Hard NEI} protocol) and explicit morphological feature modeling.

\subsection{Sparse, Dense, and Hybrid Evidence Retrieval}
Evidence retrieval architectures combine sparse inverted index methods with dense neural dual-encoders:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sparse Lexical Retrieval (BM25):} BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25} provides highly efficient lexical matching based on term frequency and inverse document frequency. However, in morphologically rich languages, BM25 fails when inflected queries diverge in surface form from corpus entries (e.g., \textit{Алматыдағы} vs. \textit{Алматының}).
    \item \textbf{Dense Semantic Retrieval:} Neural retrievers, such as DPR \citep{karpukhin2020dpr} and Contriever \citep{izacard2021contriever}, encode queries and passages into dense latent representations via contrastive dual-encoders. While effective at semantic matching, dense retrievers frequently stumble on exact numerical quantities, personal proper nouns, and low-frequency morphologically inflected names in low-resource environments \citep{clark2020tydi}.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF):} \citet{cormack2009reciprocal} introduced Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), a parameter-free algorithm that combines rankings from disparate retrieval pipelines:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rrf_formula_relwork}
    \mathrm{RRF}(d) = \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \frac{1}{k + r_m(d)}
    \end{equation}
    where $r_m(d)$ is the rank of document $d$ under model $m$, and $k \approx 60$ is a smoothing constant. RRF balances the exact entity precision of sparse lexical search with the semantic flexibility of dense neural embeddings, providing the state-of-the-art foundation for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020retrieval}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contrastive Representation Learning and Trust Geometry}
\label{sec:rel_representation_learning}

To overcome the brittle decision boundaries of cross-entropy optimization and prevent neural models from latching onto generator-specific decoding artifacts, representation learning has increasingly turned to contrastive objectives.

\subsection{Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon)}
\citet{khosla2020supervised} proposed Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon), extending self-supervised contrastive learning to fully supervised settings. In SupCon, normalized latent representations of samples belonging to the same semantic class are pulled together on the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, while samples from differing classes are actively repelled:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:supcon_loss_relwork}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SupCon}} = \sum_{i \in I} \frac{-1}{|P(i)|} \sum_{p \in P(i)} \log \frac{\exp(\bm{z}_i \cdot \bm{z}_p / \tau)}{\sum_{a \in A(i)} \exp(\bm{z}_i \cdot \bm{z}_a / \tau)}
\end{equation}
where $P(i)$ denotes the set of positive indices sharing the same class label as anchor $i$, and $\tau > 0$ is a scalar temperature parameter. Hyperspherical clustering under SupCon enforces uniform intra-class compactness and maximal inter-class separation, providing provable robustness against label noise, generator domain shifts, and out-of-distribution attacks.

\subsection{Model Calibration and Epistemic Confidence}
Modern neural networks with high classification accuracy frequently suffer from severe overconfidence \citep{guo2017calibration}. Temperature scaling provides a straightforward post-hoc calibration mechanism:
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i = \max_k \operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_i}{T}\right)_k
\end{equation}
Calibrating probabilities ensures that estimated confidence scores reflect true empirical accuracy, which is essential when deploying automated forensics in high-stakes auditing environments.

\subsection{Multidimensional Trust Modeling}
Traditional AI auditing systems operate in isolated functional silos: detection models evaluate whether a text is synthetic without considering factual truth, while fact-checkers verify factual accuracy under the implicit assumption of human authorship. When autoregressive foundation models generate fluent, highly plausible hallucinations, isolated detectors and fact-checkers fail catastrophically. 

\citet{guo2026kazakh} outlined the conceptual necessity of multidimensional trust modeling in Central Asian linguistic ecosystems. In this dissertation, we formalize this paradigm into a mathematically rigorous 2D Cartesian Trust Matrix that unifies generative provenance ($T_{\text{gen}}$) and evidence-grounded veracity ($T_{\text{fact}}$) into a joint continuous geometry.

\section{Summary of Unaddressed Research Gaps}
\label{sec:rel_gaps}

Our systematic analysis of the existing literature reveals three acute, unaddressed voids that motivate the contributions of this dissertation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Linguistic Neglect of Agglutinative Morphology in AI Forensics:} Prior AI detection research has treated text as an undifferentiated stream of statistical subwords. In agglutinative languages such as Kazakh, this induces catastrophic subword token inflation, erases root morpheme boundaries, and causes unacceptable false positive accusations on short texts.
    \item \textbf{Total Absence of Agglutinative Fact-Checking Benchmarks:} Low-resource Turkic languages lack standardized, human-validated fact verification corpora. Existing cross-encoders exploit superficial unigram overlap shortcuts, leaving systems completely defenseless against morphologically inverted claims and subtle evidential shifts.
    \item \textbf{The Provenance-Veracity Conflation Void:} No existing forensic framework simultaneously models generative provenance and factual truth. By isolating these orthogonal axes, contemporary systems remain blind to verified synthetic journalism on one hand, and malicious human disinformation on the other.
\end{enumerate}

In the subsequent chapters, we resolve each of these foundational challenges, beginning with the formal linguistic and finite-state architecture of the Kazakh language in Chapter \ref{chap:linguistic_foundation}.
